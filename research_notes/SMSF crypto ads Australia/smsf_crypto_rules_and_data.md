# Australian SMSFs holding crypto: legal rules, regulator warnings and holdings data (researched 11 October 2026)

Notes on sourcing: ato.gov.au and austrac.gov.au pages were read in full through a rendering fetcher; the plain ATO "crypto-assets-and-smsfs" URL returned 404, so the ATO's core SMSF crypto guidance was read from its public content API copy (dated 29 June 2022, still the ATO's standing guidance). Statements marked "provider claim" come from SMSF administrators or vendors, not regulators.

## 1. What the SIS Act and SIS Regulations require for SMSF crypto

### Takeaway
Crypto is a permitted SMSF investment, but only if the trust deed allows it, the written investment strategy covers it, the fund (not the member) visibly owns it in a separate wallet or exchange account, it was not acquired from a related party (so no transferring in coins you already own), and it is valued at market value every 30 June. Each of these is a separate piece of paperwork or setup.

### Cited Findings
- ATO: "While SMSFs are not prohibited from investing in crypto assets, the investment must: be allowed under the fund's trust deed; be in accordance with the fund's investment strategy; comply with the same regulatory requirements as apply to other investments" under the SIS Act and SIS Regulations. The ATO "strongly encourage[s] SMSFs to seek independent professional advice before investing in crypto assets" (ATO guidance "SMSF investing in crypto assets", dated 29 June 2022), [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c)
- Investment strategy: before investing in crypto, trustees "should consider the level of risk of the investment" and "may then review and, if necessary, update their fund's investment strategy to ensure the investment being considered is permitted"; the ATO points to SIS Reg 4.09 (operating standard: investment strategy), [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c)
- ASIC (17 January 2022): when developing and reviewing the investment strategy trustees "need to document how your fund's investments will meet your retirement goals having regard to diversification, the risks of inadequate diversification, liquidity and the ability of the fund to discharge its liabilities. You must also be able to demonstrate that the fund owns the asset.", [ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/articles/warning-self-managed-super-funds-and-crypto-investments)
- Provider claim (SMSFcentral, reviewed 12 August 2026): s52B(2)(f) of the SIS Act requires the strategy to consider risk, return, diversification, liquidity and ability to pay benefits, and Reg 4.09 requires regular review; "A strategy that gestures at 'other investments' will not carry a volatile allocation through an audit." Auditors want to see which digital assets the fund may hold, the allocation range, and how risk was assessed, [SMSFcentral](https://www.smsfcentral.com.au/smsf-cryptocurrency/)
- Separation of assets (Reg 4.09A): "An SMSF's crypto assets must be held and managed separately from the personal or business investments of trustees and members... the fund must maintain and be able to provide evidence of a separate crypto wallet for the SMSF from that used by trustees and members personally.", [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c)
- ATO (27 May 2025): "Make sure your SMSF's crypto wallet is registered in the name of your SMSF" and "Keep your personal crypto investments separate from your SMSFs assets. Failing to do this can be a breach of the Superannuation Industry (Supervision) Act 1993.", [ATO, Navigating SMSF crypto assets](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/navigating-smsf-crypto-assets)
- Provider claim: register the exchange account "in the fund's name, or in the corporate trustee's name on behalf of the fund"; hardware wallets and cold storage must be documented as fund property, with records of wallet addresses and private key arrangements. Buying through a personal exchange account and calling it the fund's "is a separation of assets breach" and "close to impossible to fix retrospectively", [SMSFcentral](https://www.smsfcentral.com.au/smsf-cryptocurrency/)
- Related parties and in-specie transfers: SMSFs are prohibited (with exceptions) from intentionally acquiring assets from related parties; the exceptions cover listed securities and business real property at market value. "Crypto assets are not 'listed securities' so don't fall within the exceptions. Therefore, crypto assets cannot be acquired from a related party. It follows that SMSF trustees and members... can't make in specie contributions or other transfers of crypto assets to the fund.", [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c)
- ATO (27 May 2025): "When transacting in crypto assets with related parties, all transactions must be done at arms-length.", [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/navigating-smsf-crypto-assets)
- Sole purpose test: the fund must be maintained solely to provide retirement (or death) benefits; it "may be a breach of the sole-purpose test where affiliate fees or commissions associated with the fund's crypto asset investment are paid to a trustee or member personally.", [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c)
- General ATO investment rules (published 2 April 2025): investments must meet the sole purpose test, be at arm's length and market value, "show clear legal ownership by the fund", and meet investment restrictions; breaching the sole purpose test means the SMSF "will not be eligible for tax concessions" and trustees "could face a range of compliance actions such as penalties.", [ATO, SMSF investment requirements](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-investing/smsf-investment-requirements)
- No personal use (provider reading of sole purpose test): it "rules out spending the fund's Bitcoin or moving tokens to a personal wallet, however temporarily", [SMSFcentral](https://www.smsfcentral.com.au/smsf-cryptocurrency/)
- Benefits: on a condition of release the SMSF can pay an in-specie lump sum in crypto (a CGT event), but "Pension payments to trustees or members, however, must be made in cash.", [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c)
- Record keeping: "When acquiring or disposing of crypto assets, SMSFs must also keep full records of their crypto transactions." For tax, crypto assets "are not a form of money but are capital gains tax (CGT) assets.", [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c)
- ATO (27 May 2025): "Record all transactions, including purchases, sales, and transfers... Sales and transfers are classified as 'disposals' and may result in capital gains tax... Also, keep information about your wallet and any changes made to it.", [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/navigating-smsf-crypto-assets)
- Provider claim: a token-to-token swap is a CGT disposal; "a year of active trading can generate hundreds of them"; staking rewards and airdrops are generally ordinary income; fund pays 15% on net gains, effectively 10% with the one-third discount after 12 months, [SMSFcentral](https://www.smsfcentral.com.au/smsf-cryptocurrency/)
- Provider claim: an SMSF cannot practically borrow to buy crypto (LRBA under s67A needs a single acquirable asset on a separate trust; "no major SMSF lender offers it"); NFTs may also fall under the collectables rules in Reg 13.18AA, [SMSFcentral](https://www.smsfcentral.com.au/smsf-cryptocurrency/)
- Breaches: trustees who think they have breached should rectify and consider the ATO's SMSF early engagement and voluntary disclosure service, [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c)
- Division 296 (provider summary): applies from 1 July 2026 to members with total super balance over $3 million (further tier above $10 million); under the Act "as passed on 10 March 2026" it taxes realised earnings, so unrealised crypto gains are not taxed under Div 296 until disposal (including swaps), but a 30 June run-up in value can lift a member's total super balance across a threshold, [SMSFcentral](https://www.smsfcentral.com.au/smsf-cryptocurrency/)

### Inferences
- Friction points a single platform could plausibly address: (1) opening an account in the fund's or corporate trustee's name, with entity verification; (2) keeping fund crypto visibly apart from the trustee's personal crypto; (3) producing a complete transaction export for CGT (swaps, staking, airdrops); (4) a consistent 30 June AUD valuation from one source each year. Shares and ETFs already have broker statements and registry records; crypto is where the paperwork gap is widest.
- The related-party rule is a strong "gotcha" for ad copy research: a trustee who already owns Bitcoin personally cannot move it into the SMSF; the fund has to buy fresh with fund money. Any ad must not suggest transferring personal crypto in.
- The investment strategy requirement means an ad should not imply "just add crypto"; trustees must document diversification and liquidity reasoning first.

### Gaps
- I did not read the SIS Regulations text on legislation.gov.au directly; Reg 4.09 and 4.09A content above is as described by the ATO and ASIC, and section numbers 52B(2)(d) and 52B(2)(f) come from a provider (SMSFcentral), not the statute itself.
- The ATO's current HTML page on SMSF crypto investing (the 2022 guidance) returned 404 at its usual URL on 11 October 2026; the content above is from the ATO's own API copy. Whether the ATO has moved or revised it in 2026 could not be confirmed.

## 2. What the ATO has said specifically about SMSFs and crypto

### Takeaway
The ATO's position is consistent from 2022 to 2025: allowed, but separate wallet in the fund's name, strategy updated, no related-party acquisitions, full records, 30 June market value backed by objective evidence. In 2025 it added warnings about theft, lost passwords and ATO impersonation scams, and told auditors that holding statements alone are not enough.

### Cited Findings
- 29 June 2022 guidance: value crypto at fair market value in AUD "obtained from a reputable digital currency exchange or website that publishes its rates publicly"; "For the purpose of calculating member balances at 30 June, the ATO will accept the 30 June closing value published on the website of a crypto exchange that reports on historical crypto values.", [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c)
- 27 May 2025 newsroom item: "With a growing number of self-managed super funds (SMSFs) investing in crypto assets... We've seen instances of SMSF trustees losing their crypto investments due to theft, lost passwords, and impersonation schemes." Tips: name the wallet correctly, separate investments, "Use legitimate platforms" (registered or licensed, independent reviews, HTTPS, clear dispute policies), keep records, protect the wallet password, keep related-party deals at arm's length, keep market valuation records for the auditor. It also warns of "impersonators posing as ATO representatives, claiming you are involved in crypto tax evasion and asking for wallet details.", [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/navigating-smsf-crypto-assets)
- 24 October 2025 newsroom item for auditors (see section 5) confirms the four checks: trust deed, investment strategy, held in a wallet in the fund's name separate from personal crypto, reported at market value, [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/auditing-smsfs-with-crypto-assets)
- Data matching (provider claim): the ATO runs a crypto data-matching program in which Australian exchanges report account holder identity, transaction amounts and wallet addresses; the provider says the ATO looks for crypto in personal accounts that should belong to the fund, unreported gains, funds holding crypto with no updated investment strategy, related-party crypto transactions, and DEX acquisitions designed to avoid reporting, [SMSFcentral](https://www.smsfcentral.com.au/smsf-cryptocurrency/). Pitcher Partners (12 September 2025) separately states the ATO has used a crypto data-matching program "Since 2019", [Pitcher Partners](https://www.pitcher.com.au/insights/making-crypto-work-for-you-and-the-australian-tax-office/)
- August 2026: the ATO released two draft rulings on crypto CGT, including "TR 2026/D1: Income tax: receipt and disposal of crypto assets by an..." (title truncated in source; SMSF relevance not confirmed), [IFPA, 21 August 2026](https://ifpa.com.au/21-august-2026)

### Inferences
- The ATO's own language ("growing number of SMSFs investing in crypto", theft and lost-password losses) supports ad messaging about custody and record keeping without making investment claims.
- "Use legitimate platforms" is an ATO recommendation, which gives an SMSF-focused platform a regulator-aligned talking point (registered, licensed, clear records), provided the ad does not imply ATO endorsement.

### Gaps
- I could not reach the ATO crypto data-matching program page (404); the specific list of what the ATO looks for comes from a provider, not the ATO.
- No ATO statement dated 2026 specific to SMSF crypto compliance focus was found. Social posts claim crypto in super is on the ATO's "active watchlist" this year, but I found no primary ATO source for that wording.

## 3. What ASIC has said (and Moneysmart)

### Takeaway
ASIC's key SMSF-specific warning dates from 17 January 2022: be wary of marketing that pushes people to switch to an SMSF to buy crypto. Its broader 2026 messaging is that crypto is high risk, many providers are still unlicensed, scams are rising (3,106 crypto scam takedowns in FY26), and a licensing regime for platforms is coming in April 2027.

### Cited Findings
- 17 January 2022: ASIC "noticed an increase in marketing recommending Australians switch from retail and industry superannuation funds to self-managed superannuation funds (SMSFs) so they can invest in a 'high return' portfolio"; "SMSF trustees are being targeted to invest in crypto-assets"; "Crypto-assets are a high risk and speculative investment"; best practice is to get advice from a licensed adviser before moving super into an SMSF. "Be wary of people 'cold calling', text messaging, or emailing you with a recommendation to transfer your super to an SMSF, or invest in crypto-assets via your SMSF." Trustees "ultimately bear responsibility for the fund's decisions and for complying with the law even if you rely on other people's advice.", [ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/articles/warning-self-managed-super-funds-and-crypto-investments)
- ASIC action cited in the same release: in November 2021 ASIC moved against unlicensed A One Multi Services Pty Ltd (Gold Coast), alleging more than $2.4 million was transferred to buy crypto-assets; its director allegedly helped investors move super into an SMSF and lend it to the company, promising returns over 20% a year, [ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/articles/warning-self-managed-super-funds-and-crypto-investments)
- Moneysmart crypto page (last updated 26 August 2026): "Most crypto are high-risk investments"; "Many crypto-asset providers are not licensed at this point in time. This means you may not be protected if the platform fails or is hacked"; "Currently there's also no public register to check the legitimacy of crypto exchanges"; "Takedowns of cryptocurrency investment scams increased by almost 30% in FY26, with ASIC removing 3,106 scams during the financial year.", [Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/complex-investment-products/crypto-assets)
- Moneysmart social post (about mid 2026, undated in snippet): ASIC warns "Australians are being targeted by high-pressure sales tactics designed to get them to switch super funds", [Moneysmart Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/MoneySmartAu/posts/watch-this-if-you-have-super-some-offers-are-not-as-simple-as-they-seem-lead-gen/1444866264349350/) (snippet only; not crypto-specific)
- ASIC roadmap (April 2026): access to the INFO 225 class no-action position "will expire in June" 2026, after which "Entities will need to comply with existing licensing requirements throughout the implementation of the law reforms.", [ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/news-items/asics-roadmap-for-digital-assets-law-reform-implementation)

### Inferences
- Ads for an SMSF crypto platform sit directly in the zone ASIC has flagged (marketing that encourages switching to an SMSF to access crypto). Copy should not urge viewers to set up or switch to an SMSF to buy crypto, should not promise returns, and should avoid cold-outreach style lead generation. This also matches the GBX house rule of no financial advice claims.

### Gaps
- I found no ASIC media release from 2024 to 2026 that is specifically about SMSF crypto promotions; the most specific ASIC SMSF crypto warning remains January 2022.
- Did not verify any ASIC enforcement action from 2024 to 2026 involving SMSF crypto promoters.

## 4. AUSTRAC: exchange registration and what it means for trustees

### Takeaway
Any business exchanging crypto for money must be registered with AUSTRAC (since 2018). From 31 March 2026, reforms renamed digital currency exchanges as "virtual asset service providers" (VASPs) and widened the regulated services to crypto-to-crypto swaps, custody (safekeeping), transfers and offer-related services. AUSTRAC registration is anti-money-laundering registration, not a consumer protection licence, and there is no public register for consumers to check.

### Cited Findings
- "Any business that provides digital currency exchange or virtual asset services must be registered with AUSTRAC." AUSTRAC can refuse, suspend, cancel or refuse to renew registration where a business "poses an unacceptable risk of money laundering, terrorism financing, or other serious crime." Recent cancellations include BA Digital Ventures Pty Ltd (4 June 2026) and several others in January to March 2026; suspensions include Cryptolink Pty Ltd (9 August 2026); FTX Express Pty Ltd was cancelled 28 June 2024. Page last updated 25 September 2026, [AUSTRAC](https://www.austrac.gov.au/about-us/record-our-actions/virtual-asset-registration-actions)
- "The exchange of virtual assets for money (and vice versa) has been regulated under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism Financing Act 2006 since 2018." New or amended designated services: exchange crypto for money (item 50A), crypto for crypto (50B), virtual asset safekeeping including control of private keys (46A), transfers on behalf of customers (items 29 to 30), and services connected with a token offer (50C). "The terms 'virtual asset' and 'virtual asset service provider' have replaced the previous terms 'digital currency' and 'digital currency exchange provider'." Page last updated 10 July 2026, [AUSTRAC](https://www.austrac.gov.au/new-austrac/designated-services-newly-regulated-entities/virtual-asset-designated-services)
- Reforms "started 31 March 2026 unless deferred under the transitional rules", [AUSTRAC, About the AML/CTF reforms](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/about-amlctf-reforms/about-reforms) (search snippet). Existing registered DCEs were "automatically registered as a virtual asset service provider (VASP) from 31 March 2026", [AUSTRAC transitional rules 2026](https://www.austrac.gov.au/about-us/legislation/updates-legislation/amlctf-transitional-rules-2026) (search snippet). Under transitional rules, businesses newly captured that applied before 29 July 2026 can keep providing the new services until AUSTRAC decides, [AUSTRAC](https://www.austrac.gov.au/new-austrac/register-us/register-us-remittance-or-virtual-asset-service-provider) (search snippet)
- Travel rule: reporting of transfers involving unverified self-hosted wallets starts 31 March 2029, [AUSTRAC](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/obligations-and-guidance/additional-guidance/travel-rule/additional-travel-rule-obligations-when-transferring-virtual-assets) (search snippet)
- Exchanges must complete customer due diligence before providing a designated service, [AUSTRAC](https://www.austrac.gov.au/new-austrac/designated-services-newly-regulated-entities/virtual-asset-designated-services)
- Moneysmart (26 August 2026): "there's also no public register to check the legitimacy of crypto exchanges", [Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/complex-investment-products/crypto-assets)

### Inferences
- For trustees: choosing an AUSTRAC-registered VASP is the baseline the ATO's "use legitimate platforms" tip points toward, but trustees cannot easily verify it themselves (no public register) and must ask the provider. SMSF onboarding also means entity KYC on the trustee (individual trustees or corporate trustee and its directors), which is more paperwork than a personal account.
- AUSTRAC registration says nothing about custody safety or insolvency protection; that gap is what the 2027 ASIC licensing regime is meant to fill.

### Gaps
- Three AUSTRAC points above rely on search-result snippets from austrac.gov.au rather than full-page reads (transitional auto-registration, 29 July 2026 date, 2029 travel rule date).
- I found no AUSTRAC guidance written specifically for SMSF trustees.

## 5. Auditor expectations and common audit problems

### Takeaway
Every SMSF is audited every year, and for crypto the auditor must confirm the deed permits it, the strategy covers it, the asset sits in a wallet in the fund's name separate from personal crypto, and it is at market value backed by more than the exchange's own holding statement. If the auditor cannot verify existence, ownership or value, the audit report is qualified and a contravention report may follow. This is the single biggest annual paperwork burden for crypto-holding funds.

### Cited Findings
- ATO to auditors (24 October 2025): confirm the investment "is permitted under the fund's trust deed"; "aligns with the fund's investment strategy, and the trustee has considered the risks and how the investment supports the member's retirement goals"; "is held and owned by the fund, that is, it is stored in a digital wallet in the fund's name, separate from any crypto personally held by trustees or members"; and "is reported at market value in the fund's financial statements.", [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/auditing-smsfs-with-crypto-assets)
- Same item: "Holding statements or investment summaries alone are not sufficient to confirm market value. You must obtain additional objective, supportable evidence", for example "the 30 June closing value published on the website of a crypto exchange that provides historical data." Conclusions must be documented in the audit file, [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/auditing-smsfs-with-crypto-assets)
- Same item: "If the crypto is held by a custodian, such as an exchange, you should obtain a Type 2 report if available and perform further substantive testing to confirm the holding statement is correct.", [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/auditing-smsfs-with-crypto-assets)
- Same item: "If you can't verify the crypto asset exists, belongs to the fund, or is reported at market value, you must qualify both Part A and Part B of the audit report if material. When the reporting criteria applies, lodge an Auditor Contravention Report (ACR) for a regulation 8.02B breach.", [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/auditing-smsfs-with-crypto-assets)
- Provider claim: the market value requirement sits in SIS Reg 8.02B; trustees should record the exchange used, the time of the price snapshot and the AUD equivalent, and use the same source each year ("Take the price from a different exchange each year and the auditor will ask why"). Illiquid tokens, NFTs and DeFi positions may need independent valuation and "will hold up the audit every year.", [SMSFcentral](https://www.smsfcentral.com.au/smsf-cryptocurrency/)
- Provider claim: five most common problems: crypto bought in a personal account later claimed as a fund asset; a strategy that never mentions digital assets; no 30 June market value record or unsupported estimate; swaps, staking and DeFi yield left out of the return; personal crypto transferred into the fund without documentation or arm's-length price. Self-custody needs a documented key storage and recovery process; multi-signature can require more than one trustee to authorise, [SMSFcentral](https://www.smsfcentral.com.au/smsf-cryptocurrency/)
- Provider claim (SMSF Coach, 27 April 2026, snippet): auditors verify the exchange account "is set up in the name of the fund" and the wallet was "purchased using funds from the SMSF", [SMSF Coach](https://smsfcoach.com.au/category/retirement-planning/pensions/)

### Inferences
- A platform that issues fund-name statements, full transaction exports, a 30 June AUD valuation, and (ideally) an independent assurance report such as a Type 2 controls report directly answers the ATO's stated audit evidence list. The ATO's statement that holding statements alone are not enough means "audit-ready reports" is a meaningful but carefully phrased claim; the auditor still needs independent price evidence.
- Self-custody (hardware wallet) shifts the burden onto the trustee to prove the wallet belongs to the fund and that keys are controlled by the trustees; this is real friction.

### Gaps
- I found no published statistic on how many SMSF audits are qualified or how many ACRs are lodged because of crypto.
- The ATO's private key control evidence expectations are not spelled out in the 2025 auditor item; key-control points above come from providers.

## 6. Data: how much crypto SMSFs hold, how many funds, and growth

### Takeaway
Crypto is a small slice of SMSF wealth: ATO estimates put it at about $5.0 billion, or 0.45% of $1.107 trillion in total SMSF assets, at June 2026. Only about 2.2% to 2.6% of SMSFs hold crypto, but those that do hold a lot of it (median holding about $81,000), and it is concentrated in small, younger funds. Note the source conflict on the headline figure explained below.

### Cited Findings
- Sector size (ATO, published 8 September 2026, June 2026 quarter): 680,301 SMSFs; 1,246,552 members; total estimated assets $1.107 trillion; listed shares 26% and cash and term deposits 16% of assets; 74% of members aged 50 or over. 2024 to 25 annual data: average assets per SMSF $1.7 million, member contributions $23.0 billion, [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/highlights-smsf-quarterly-statistical-report-june-2026)
- Earlier quarters: March 2026, 672,805 SMSFs and $1.06 trillion (published 16 June 2026); December 2025, 663,867 SMSFs and $1.06 trillion (published 13 March 2026), [ATO March 2026](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/highlights-smsf-quarterly-statistical-report-march-2026); [ATO December 2025](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/highlights-smsf-quarterly-statistical-report-december-2025)
- Crypto, June 2026 quarter: crypto assets $5,010 million, 0.45% of total SMSF assets of $1,107,175 million (table reproduced from the ATO SMSF quarterly statistical report, June 2026), [SuperGuide, updated 15 September 2026](https://www.superguide.com.au/smsfs/smsf-investment-diy-super-asset-types); same $5.01 billion figure and 0.45% cited from the June 2026 ATO report by [Thomas Prizeman, LinkedIn, October 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/thomas-prizeman-421245151_for-all-the-noise-about-crypto-in-super-activity-7512987911907135489-UW09)
- Growth: SuperGuide says crypto was "0.45% of all SMSF assets in June 2026, worth $5.0 billion, down slightly from $5.2 billion a year earlier but around three times the level of March 2024.", [SuperGuide](https://www.superguide.com.au/smsfs/smsf-investment-diy-super-asset-types)
- CONFLICT: the December 2025 ATO report was reported as showing crypto of $3.25 billion (0.3% of total assets), [autoSMSF, May 2026](https://www.autosmsf.com.au/quarterly-ato-statistics-update-2026); the same $3.25 billion "held directly in digital assets by SMSFs, a figure that excludes exposure through ETFs and managed funds" was cited in May 2026, [SMSF Adviser](https://www.smsfadviser.com/coinbase-enters-smsf-sector-with-new-solution/). Cryptonews (9 October 2026) says crypto holdings "slipped 4% in the year to June 2025, earlier ATO data showed", [Cryptonews](https://cryptonews.com.au/news/australian-smsf-investors-push-crypto-buy-sell-ratio-to-record-high-135165/). These do not reconcile with SuperGuide's $5.2 billion at June 2025.

### Inferences
- The most defensible headline for ad research is "about $5 billion, under half a percent of SMSF assets (ATO estimate, June 2026)". Because ATO quarterly estimates were revised with the 2024 to 25 annual data, any figure should be quoted with its quarter and report date, and older $3 billion to $3.25 billion figures should not be mixed with the newer series.
- The audience is narrow but deep: few funds hold crypto, but holders have large positions and skew toward smaller, younger, accumulation-phase funds; new SMSF members skew 35 to 49. This aligns with a platform pitch to younger trustees who want shares, ETFs and crypto in one place.
- Competitive note: Coinbase is already pitching the same "one place, fewer logins" message to SMSF trustees as of May 2026.

## 7. 2024 to 2026 regulatory changes affecting crypto custody and exchanges

### Takeaway
Two big changes: AUSTRAC's AML/CTF reforms started 31 March 2026 (broader VASP regime), and the Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026 brings digital asset platforms and tokenised custody platforms under AFS licensing from 9 April 2027, with ASIC setting custody (asset-holding) standards. For SMSF trustees this means licensed, ASIC-supervised crypto platforms from 2027, which should make the ATO's "use legitimate platforms" advice easier to satisfy.

### Cited Findings
- The Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026 "passed Parliament on 1 April 2026, received Royal Assent on 8 April 2026, and will commence on 9 April 2027", bringing digital asset platforms (DAPs) and tokenised custody platforms (TCPs) "under the financial services licensing regime from April 2027" with an 18-month implementation timeline, [ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/news-items/asics-roadmap-for-digital-assets-law-reform-implementation)
- ASIC will consult on asset-holding standards (s912BE, based on RG 133 custody standards), transactional and settlement standards (s912BF), and financial requirements (like RG 166); a new DAP/TCP Regulatory Guide in months 6 to 12; licence applications open in months 12 to 18 with operators able to trade under relief while applications are processed, [ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/news-items/asics-roadmap-for-digital-assets-law-reform-implementation)
- Gilbert + Tobin (9 April 2026): DAP and TCP operators must hold an AFSL, follow ASIC asset-holding, transaction and settlement standards, and publish a platform guide, voting policy and rules; low-value exemption where transactions are no more than $10 million over 12 months; certain custodial staking arrangements are not managed investment schemes or financial products; six-month transition from commencement for providers without an AFSL, extended until ASIC decides if an application is lodged, [Gilbert + Tobin](https://www.gtlaw.com.au/insights/key-topics/regulation-in-motion/australia-passes-highly-anticipated-digital-asset-regulation)
- ASIC INFO 225 class no-action position expired in June 2026; entities must comply with existing licensing requirements during implementation, [ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/news-items/asics-roadmap-for-digital-assets-law-reform-implementation)
- AUSTRAC AML/CTF reforms started 31 March 2026 (VASP renaming, expanded services including safekeeping and crypto-to-crypto exchange), [AUSTRAC](https://www.austrac.gov.au/new-austrac/designated-services-newly-regulated-entities/virtual-asset-designated-services); [AUSTRAC reforms page](https://www.austrac.gov.au/industry-and-business/about-amlctf-reforms/about-reforms)
- Coinbase Australia's SMSF product launched after it received an AFSL from ASIC; SMSF Adviser notes the launch "comes weeks after the passage of the Corporations Amendment (Digital Asset Platforms) Bill" [sic, the Digital Assets Framework Bill], [SMSF Adviser, 5 May 2026](https://www.smsfadviser.com/coinbase-enters-smsf-sector-with-new-solution/)
- Other super changes 2026 (context only): Division 296 tax on balances over $3 million from 1 July 2026 (passed 10 March 2026 per provider), [SMSFcentral](https://www.smsfcentral.com.au/smsf-cryptocurrency/); new LRBAs for residential property effectively removed from 10 August 2026 (new LRBA property must generally be business real property), [SuperGuide](https://www.superguide.com.au/smsfs/smsf-investment-diy-super-asset-types)

### Inferences
- Between now (October 2026) and full implementation after April 2027, many crypto platforms are AUSTRAC-registered but not yet licensed under the new DAP regime; an ad should not claim a platform is "ASIC-licensed for crypto custody" unless it actually holds the relevant AFSL authorisation.
- The LRBA residential change (August 2026) may push some younger accumulation-phase trustees away from leveraged residential property toward listed assets, ETFs and possibly crypto; this is speculative and unsupported by data so far.

### Gaps
- Treasury's own materials were not read directly; licensing details come from ASIC and a law firm summary.
- No source found on whether ASIC's custody standards will have SMSF-specific requirements (for example, segregated accounts in the trustee's name).
