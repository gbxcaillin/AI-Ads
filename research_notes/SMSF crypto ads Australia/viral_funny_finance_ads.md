## Australian examples: crypto exchanges, investing apps, super funds, banks and neobanks. What was the joke, why did it work, and any measurable results?

### Takeaway
In Australia the funny finance work that has measurable results comes from super funds and banks, not crypto exchanges: Australian Retirement Trust's monster mascot (2024) is the best-evidenced case (2.9x category ad attention, Effie-recognised), and ubank's crayon "Cheap Ad" (2024) and Spirit Super's procrastination skits (2023) show the low-cost and comedian-led routes. Australian crypto exchanges (Swyftx and peers) have run aspirational rather than comic brand work, so a funny SMSF-crypto spot would have little direct local competition in tone.

### Cited Findings
**Super funds**
- Australian Retirement Trust (ART) "Awaken Your Super", April 2024: creative by M&C Saatchi, media by Bohemia, directed by The Glue Society; the idea treats super as a "monster" so powerful people should take control of it ([Mediaweek](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/brands/mc-saatchi-and-bohemia-unveil-awaken-your-super-for-australian-retirement-trust); [Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/?p=258894); [mi-3](https://mi-3.com.au/29-04-2024/australian-retirement-trust-awakens-monster-new-campaign-mc-saatchi-group)).
- ART results: trade press reports the campaign created 2.9 times the ad attention and brand consideration impact versus the superannuation category average, and an Effie Australia case study credits it with a 126% YoY increase in ROI; ART's marketing lead said the board and CEO were not initially on board with a monster mascot and the bolder "blue pill" route was the one that ran ([AdNews](https://adnews.com.au/news/how-australian-retirement-trust-s-big-blue-monster-led-to-a-business-boom); [Effies via Advertising Council](https://advertisingcouncil.org.au/effies/?p=31041)). Media later moved to EssenceMediacom in July 2025 after Bohemia was shuttered (same AdNews source).
- Spirit Super "Don't Be Super Stupid", launched around 2 June 2023 via Common Ventures: built with comedian Alex Ward around "moments of extreme procrastination", targeting people who will do anything except sort out their super; ran across BVOD, social, digital video, display and podcasts ([Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/?p=239889); [AdNews](https://www.adnews.com.au/campaigns/spirit-super-s-don-t-be-super-stupid-via-common-ventures); [Owler/Financial Standard listing](https://www.owler.com/company/spiritsuper)). An LBB agency page lists 30-second spots titled "Seance", "Alan" and "Nail" under the banner ([LBB Common Ventures work](https://lbbonline.com/profile/Common-Ventures/work)). Spirit Super merged into CareSuper on 1 November 2024 ([MTA NSW](https://mtansw.com.au/services-resources/business-partners-pages/spirit-super)).
- Rest Super "Super. Simple." via Reunion, March 2024, then an October 2024 iteration adding a uniformed character, the "Rest Representative", who explains super in plain terms ([Mumbrella, Mar 2024](https://mumbrella.com.au/its-totally-fresh-for-the-industry-both-in-execution-and-experience-rest-super-launches-new-campaign-via-reunion-820862); [Mumbrella, Oct 2024](https://mumbrella.com.au/rest-super-supercharges-brand-platform-with-new-iteration-via-reunion-854386)).
- Industry Super Funds "Compare the Pair": originated 2005, relaunched 2014 (10-year net returns), third generation 2017, refreshed 2023 to compare 3, 10 and 15-year outcomes with an illustrated switcher more than $54,000 better off after 15 years; ASIC raised confusion concerns in 2005 (campaign changed and suspended, then relaunched under an agreement barring certain projections and fee-only comparisons) and again in 2014 (ISA agreed to clarify sample definitions) ([SMSF Adviser](https://www.smsfadviser.com/compare-the-pair-campaign-resurfaces); [Super Review](https://www.superreview.com.au/isa-compares-pair-again); [Super Review feature](https://superreview.com.au/features-analysis/expert-analysis/compare-these-ads-2008)). The current comparison methodology (SuperRatings, 5, 10 and 15 years to 30 June 2025) is published on an assumptions page ([Industry Super assumptions](https://www.industrysuper.com/footer/assumptions)). Critics argue it is not a like-for-like comparison (same SMSF Adviser source).

**Banks and neobanks**
- ubank "Cheap Ad, for a Great Rate", 10 July 2024, made by ubank's in-house studio: a deliberately cheap, crayon-and-highlighter ad following a hapless social media manager using old-school tactics, announcing a 5.5% p.a. max bonus rate; the joke is the contrast between a market-leading rate and messy scribbles ("a few crayons and a highlighter was more within our budget", creative director Callam Hanks). Rolled out as lo-fi social (TikTok, Meta, Reddit) with inflatable tubes, sign spinners, photoshopped Times Square billboards, human billboards, discounted Sydney trains buys, and Spotify ads voiced by the marketing team. ubank was the most-followed Australian bank on TikTok (175,400). No campaign performance figures reported ([The Stable](https://www.thestable.com.au/ubank-cheap-ads-to-woo-australian-savers/)).
- Up "#UpEasyMoney" (early 2021): Australia's first gamified TikTok effect, where users "munch" as many coins as possible in 30 seconds, with a prize running to 31 March 2021; Up said "having a sense of humour and being good with money aren't mutually exclusive" ([Mozo](https://www.mozo.com.au/neobanks/articles/up-teams-up-with-tiktok-to-release-australia-s-first-ever-gamified-tiktok-effect)).

**Investing apps**
- Superhero "The Floor Is Yours" (Hardhat): Superhero's first brand TVC, debuting 4 June during Channel 7's AFL broadcast and running every game to the Grand Final, part of a $1 million partnership ([Mumbrella](https://mumbrella.com.au/fintech-startup-superhero-launches-first-major-brand-tvc-686234); [Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/?p=203834); [B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/?p=1351881)). An earlier Hardhat communications campaign "gives trading a kick up the ASX" with cheeky taglines ([Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/?p=192923)).
- Stake "The Takeover" (Feb 2022, by Bear Meets Eagle On Fire): 1930s Fleischer-style animation depicting the inaccessibility of traditional Wall Street ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/bear-meets-eagle-on-fire-stake-the-takeover-advertising-080222)).

**Crypto exchanges**
- Swyftx's 2024 video, audio and OOH campaign (Landor and Wavemaker) is about "pursuing simple dreams in big ways" and making financial freedom "feel more human", featuring rockstar mums and novice golfers ([AdNews](https://www.adnews.com.au/campaigns/swyftx-unveils-first-campaign-since-brand-refresh-via-landor-and-wavemaker)). A later in-house campaign "Where can crypto take you" explores everyday monotony and the desire for change, airing on free-to-air and Kayo, 9Now, Binge and Paramount+ ([Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/?p=292814)). Neither is positioned as comedy.

### Inferences
- The Australian proof points for "funny finance" sit in super and banking, where the joke targets apathy or the brand itself (procrastination, a monster you must tame, a cheap-looking ad), never the product's returns. That is the safest pattern to borrow.
- ART shows a mascot can survive a conservative board and still be measurable; it also suggests category benchmarks (attention vs category average) are the metric to brief against.
- ubank's self-deprecation is the closest analogue to an SMSF hassle ad: the brand mocks process and production values while the hard claim (the rate) stays plain.
- The absence of comic Australian crypto exchange brand work (in what I found) suggests both a white space and a sign that compliance teams in AU crypto have avoided humour.

### Gaps
- No trade-press coverage found of comic campaigns from CoinSpot, Independent Reserve, CoinJar, Crypto.com AU, Raiz, Pearler, Spaceship, CommSec Pocket, Sharesies AU, ING, Hostplus, Aware or Cbus within the searches run; this is a search gap, not proof none exist.
- No view counts or measured results found for Spirit Super, Rest, ubank, Up, Superhero or Stake campaigns.
- Superhero TVC year not confirmed from the snippets (Mumbrella post id suggests 2021, unverified). The Swyftx "Where can crypto take you" date is not stated in the snippet.
- I found no specific Australian finance ad that verifiably went viral on TikTok, Instagram or YouTube with published view counts.

## International examples worth borrowing, and which aged badly or drew regulatory complaints

### Takeaway
The best international model of attention without a risk claim is Coinbase's 2022 QR-code Super Bowl spot (format gimmick, no promise, measurable spike); the ones that aged badly (Crypto.com "Fortune favours the brave", FTX with Larry David, Coinbase UK "Everything Is Fine") all tied crypto to bravery, missing out, or escape from hardship. Fintech comedy that works (Wise, Cash App, Chime) mocks banks, fees and everyday money moments rather than promising outcomes.

### Cited Findings
**Worked**
- Coinbase QR code, Super Bowl LVI (13 Feb 2022): a 60 s bouncing QR code; Coinbase's case study reported 20m+ site visits during the 60 s ad, 445k+ new sign-ups, 14B+ media impressions and +300% brand consideration; the app jumped from 186th to 2nd on the US App Store; the site crashed under traffic; reported slot cost about $13 to 14m; offer was $15 free bitcoin plus a $3m giveaway ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/linked/134101); [TechCrunch/Sensor Tower](https://techcrunch.com/2022/02/17/super-bowl-ads-boosted-crypto-app-downloads-by-279-led-by-coinbase/amp); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2022/02/18/coinbase-super-bowl-coup-helps-push-crypto-app-downloads-up-279); [Coinbase case study summary](https://www.llllitl.fr/2022/06/coinbase-less-talk-more-bitcoin/)). Sensor Tower measured installs up 309% week on week on broadcast day; Coinbase, eToro and FTX US installs grew a collective 279% on 13 Feb ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/02/17/super-bowl-ads-boosted-crypto-app-downloads-by-279-led-by-coinbase/amp)).
- Wise (with Little Big Engine): absurdist spots poking fun at outdated money-transfer habits on TV, CTV, YouTube and radio; another Wise spot follows a banker through the wreckage of an office party paid for by bank fees ([DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/little-big-engine-wise-be-smart-and-get-wise); [Moloco blog](https://www.moloco.com/vi/blog/fintech-pr-campaigns-that-worked)). Date and metrics not given.
- Cash App "Cash In", launched 17 March 2025: four-part comic series directed by Ramy Youssef ([Everything PR](https://everything-pr.com/cash-apps-cash-in-campaign-why-ramy-youssef-directing-your-ads-is-smarter-than-s); [shots](https://magazine.shots.net/news/view/cash-apps-financial-fun)).
- Chime "Bank Smarter This Season", launched 22 October 2025: Jason Momoa plays a rotating set of everyday characters ([Everything PR](https://everything-pr.com/chimes-bank-smarter-campaign-with-jason-momoa-a-playbook-for-fintech-pr-that-act)).

**Aged badly or drew complaints**
- Crypto.com "Fortune Favours the Brave" (debuted Oct 2021, Matt Damon): compared crypto to climbing mountains and space travel without explaining crypto; immediate backlash and ridicule, renewed when prices fell to 16-month lows in 2022; critics called it a tactic for goading men into risking savings ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/89680/good-shill-hunting-why-matt-damon-crypto-com-ad-is-trending); [TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/matt-damon-attacked-after-crypto-bomb/); [The Brag](https://thebrag.com/matt-damon-faces-backlash-after-crypto-crash-for-fortune-favors-the-brave)).
- FTX, Larry David Super Bowl ad (Feb 2022): FTX filed for bankruptcy in November 2022; a class action named David alongside Tom Brady and others, alleging no due diligence; the celebrities called the theory "nonsense"; David said in January 2024 "So, like an idiot, I did it" and that he "lost a lot of money" ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/ftx-larry-david-super-bowl-ad); [AFS Law](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/tom-brady-larry-david-and-others-named-defendants-class-action-suit-filed); [Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/news/larry-david-regrets-ftx-super-bowl-ad-participation)).
- Coinbase UK "Everything Is Fine" (ran August 2025; ASA ruling about 29 January 2026): working adults sing a cheerful satirical song about rising costs, unaffordable homes and job losses; posters read "HOME OWNERSHIP OUT OF REACH", "EGGS NOW OUT OF BUDGET", "REAL WAGES STUCK IN 2008". The ASA upheld 35 complaints, finding the ads "trivialised the risks associated with cryptocurrency investment" by implying crypto is an alternative to financial hardship (CAP Code rule 1.3, social responsibility). Coinbase argued it was obvious satire and pointed to onboarding risk warnings and cooling-off periods ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/356156/uk-regulator-bans-coinbase-ads-over-cost-of-living-messaging); [Trade Informer](https://tradeinformer.com/broker-news/british-watchdog-bans-irresponsible-coinbase-ads-presenting-crypto-as-an-alternative-to-yookay-misery); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/uk-regulator-bans-coinbase-ads-143604667.html)). Note: Decrypt dates the ads August 2024; other sources say 2025.
- Revolut Valentine's billboards (Feb 2019, pre-range anchor): "single-shaming" data jokes drew complaints; the ASA referred data concerns to the FCA, found no code breach within its scope; Revolut admitted the figures were made up and should have been labelled fictitious ([Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/data-and-privacy/revolut-ad-passed-fca)).

### Inferences
- The Coinbase QR spot is borrowable because the humour is in the format (a screensaver-style bounce) and it carried no return claim; a crypto ad can be playful about the medium without being playful about risk.
- The three failures share one mechanism: the joke made crypto the hero that saves you from fear, hardship or missing out. Satire that frames crypto as an escape from cost-of-living pain was ruled irresponsible even though everyone agreed it was satire.
- Celebrity casting magnifies downside: when the asset or the firm falls, the celebrity line becomes the meme.

### Gaps
- Not researched in depth within the tool budget: Robinhood, Monzo, Kraken brand work, eToro, Public.com, NerdWallet, Mint, Credit Karma and PensionBee comic campaigns. No ASA ruling against PensionBee or Monzo was found ([search result summary via Osborne Clarke page above](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/data-and-privacy/revolut-ad-passed-fca)).
- Outcome of the FTX celebrity class action not confirmed.
- No view counts found for Wise, Cash App or Chime spots.

## Comedy formats that work in finance, and which suit a 15 to 30 second social cut versus a 30 second hero

### Takeaway
The formats with evidence behind them are: a character or mascot (ART monster, Rest Representative), comedian-led procrastination skits (Spirit Super, 30 s spots), self-deprecating lo-fi (ubank), absurd exaggeration of the old way (Wise, Stake animation), deadpan side-by-side (Compare the Pair) and a format gimmick (Coinbase QR). For an SMSF crypto hassle ad, the strongest borrowable structure is "mock the process, not the asset": an exaggerated montage of paperwork and friction, a deadpan explainer or mascot, and a plain, unfunny compliance line.

### Cited Findings
- Character mascot: ART's monster stands in for super's power and asks people to take control of it, with measured attention and ROI uplift ([AdNews](https://adnews.com.au/news/how-australian-retirement-trust-s-big-blue-monster-led-to-a-business-boom)); Rest added a uniformed "Rest Representative" character to explain super plainly ([Mumbrella](https://mumbrella.com.au/rest-super-supercharges-brand-platform-with-new-iteration-via-reunion-854386)).
- Relatable-pain procrastination skits at 30 s ("Seance", "Alan", "Nail") with a comedian collaborator, run on BVOD, social and podcasts ([Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/?p=239889); [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/profile/Common-Ventures/work)).
- Self-deprecating lo-fi plus stunts, adapted per channel (TikTok, Meta, Reddit, Spotify, OOH) ([The Stable](https://www.thestable.com.au/ubank-cheap-ads-to-woo-australian-savers/)).
- Absurd exaggeration and satire of the incumbent: Wise's absurdist spots and the banker-in-the-wrecked-office-party spot ([DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/little-big-engine-wise-be-smart-and-get-wise); [Moloco](https://www.moloco.com/vi/blog/fintech-pr-campaigns-that-worked)); Stake's 1930s animation of old Wall Street ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/bear-meets-eagle-on-fire-stake-the-takeover-advertising-080222)).
- Deadpan side-by-side comparison: Compare the Pair, but every comparison claim invites ASIC scrutiny over methodology ([SMSF Adviser](https://www.smsfadviser.com/compare-the-pair-campaign-resurfaces)).
- Format gimmick with no claim: Coinbase QR code ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/linked/134101)).
- Interactive or gamified social: Up's TikTok effect ([Mozo](https://www.mozo.com.au/neobanks/articles/up-teams-up-with-tiktok-to-release-australia-s-first-ever-gamified-tiktok-effect)).
- Short-form disclosure constraint: the ASA found a 1 s risk warning at the start of a 20 s Kraken ad, too dense to read, was insufficient ([Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102henu/asa-clamps-down-on-crypto-asset-advertising); [ASA ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/payward-ltd-a21-1121872-payward-ltd.html)).

### Inferences
- 15 to 30 s social cut: relatable-pain "POV" or montage of frustration (forms, ID checks, trustee minutes, wallet setup, the accountant's email) works best because the joke lands in the first 2 to 3 seconds without needing setup; a recurring character lets a series of 15 s cuts each carry one pain point.
- 30 s hero: a character or deadpan explainer gives space for the turn (chaos, then the calm path) and leaves the last 5 to 7 seconds for the end card and a legible risk line held on screen, not flashed (per the Kraken ruling logic).
- Split-screen before and after is attractive for "hassle vs easy" but drifts toward comparison claims; keep it about time and steps, not returns or fees, unless the claim is substantiated.
- Patterns to borrow for an SMSF crypto hassle ad: (1) the butt of the joke is admin and process, never the asset or non-investors; (2) humour lives in exaggeration of effort (mountain of paperwork, trustee meeting as a seance, a mascot buried in forms); (3) no implied returns, no FOMO ("don't miss out"), no "escape your money worries" framing; (4) a voiced and on-screen general-advice and risk statement with readable dwell time; (5) a non-celebrity or comedian-as-character casting so the line cannot become a meme if prices fall; (6) a structure that suits a series (one pain point per cut).

### Gaps
- No published A/B or effectiveness data comparing humour formats in finance specifically was retrieved (The Financial Brand discusses the question but was not fetched: [The Financial Brand](https://thefinancialbrand.com/news/bank-marketing/funny-banking-advertising-campaign-strategy-68994)).
- No Australian evidence found on optimal length (15 vs 30 s) for finance ads.

## Where humour went wrong in finance ads: trivialising risk, implied returns, FOMO, celebrity hype, mocking non-investors, and ASIC or ASA rulings

### Takeaway
The documented failures are UK ASA rulings (Coinbase "Everything Is Fine" 2026 for trivialising risk; a 2021 batch of six crypto exchange bans including Kraken, Coinbase, Luno and eToro for weak risk warnings) and reputational and legal fallout from celebrity hype (Crypto.com, FTX). In Australia the enforcement record is ASIC action on misleading crypto claims (Bobbob 2023, Qoin) rather than on humour per se, and the updated RG 234 (9 June 2026) now explicitly covers finfluencers, short-form video and AI-generated ads, which directly applies to an AI-generated spot.

### Cited Findings
- UK ASA, Coinbase "Everything Is Fine": 35 complaints upheld; ads must not appear again in that form; ruled to trivialise crypto investment risk by implying crypto as an alternative to hardship ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/356156/uk-regulator-bans-coinbase-ads-over-cost-of-living-messaging); [Trade Informer](https://tradeinformer.com/broker-news/british-watchdog-bans-irresponsible-coinbase-ads-presenting-crypto-as-an-alternative-to-yookay-misery)).
- UK ASA crypto batch (2021 rulings): six bans against exchanges Coinbase Europe, Kraken, Coinburp, Luno, EXMO and eToro; Kraken's 20 s billboard showed its caution for one second; the ASA then issued crypto ad guidance ([Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102henu/asa-clamps-down-on-crypto-asset-advertising); [ASA Payward ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/payward-ltd-a21-1121872-payward-ltd.html); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=1206651); [DAC Beachcroft](https://www.dacbeachcroft.com/en/What-we-think/Would-you-like-Bitcoin-toppings-on-your-pizza-UK-Advertising-watchdog-issues-rulings-against-crypto)). A Papa John's Bitcoin pizza promotion in the same batch was not found misleading (Burges Salmon).
- eToro was separately criticised for Yahoo Finance ads that "failed to illustrate the risk of the investment"; eToro said a risk warning was omitted through a review lapse ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=1206651)).
- ASIC, Bobbob (September 2023): paid A$53,280 in infringement notices over representations about ASIC approval, safety and interest rate of a crypto-linked product, plus a 12-month enforceable undertaking ([ASIC 23-261MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2023-releases/23-261mr-fintech-company-pays-penalties-for-crypto-product-representations)).
- ASIC v BPS Financial (Qoin): alleged ads were misleading or deceptive, including claims Qoin was regulated or approved in Australia ([B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/asic-alleges-ads-for-qoin-crypto-asset-were-deceptive/)).
- ASIC RG 234 reissued 9 June 2026: consolidates RG 53 (past performance), drops "good practice" from the title, clarifies the law applies to AI-generated advertising and intermediaries, treats influencers with active involvement like promoters; separately a ban on advertising super funds during employee onboarding starts 1 July 2026 ([ASIC RG 234](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/regulatory-guides/rg-234-advertising-financial-products-and-services-including-credit); [Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/asic-tightens-advertising-guidance-what-financial-and-credit-service-providers-need-to-know/); [AdNews](https://www.adnews.com.au/news/asic-updates-financial-advertising-guidance)).
- Industry Super's Compare the Pair twice modified after ASIC concerns (2005, 2014) ([SMSF Adviser](https://www.smsfadviser.com/compare-the-pair-campaign-resurfaces)).
- Celebrity hype: Crypto.com and FTX cases above ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/matt-damon-attacked-after-crypto-bomb/); [AFS Law](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/tom-brady-larry-david-and-others-named-defendants-class-action-suit-filed)).

### Inferences
- "It was obviously satire" is not a defence: the ASA accepted Coinbase's ad was satirical and still banned it. An Australian reviewer applying RG 234's net-impression test would likely reason similarly.
- For a GBX-style SMSF spot made with AI generation, RG 234's explicit coverage of AI-generated ads means the generated content carries the same misleading-conduct exposure as filmed content.
- Mocking non-investors or implying the viewer is foolish for not holding crypto would combine the FOMO problem (FTX's "don't miss out" framing) with tone risk; Spirit Super's "super stupid" line works because it targets procrastination about one's own admin, not a choice of asset.

### Gaps
- No Australian Ad Standards determination against a funny finance or crypto ad was found; the case database would need direct searching.
- No ASIC action found that targets humour specifically. Crypto-specific sections of RG 234 not confirmed.
- No Crypto.com ASA ruling confirmed in these searches.
- Status of Qoin case and Kraken ruling exact date not verified beyond the 2021 batch reporting.

## Currently running Meta/TikTok ads from Australian crypto exchanges and investing apps (AdWhispr)

### Takeaway
AdWhispr was available but does not track the relevant brands: searches for Swyftx, CoinSpot, Stake and Coinbase returned no tracked brand. Per instructions, nothing was added, saved or launched.
