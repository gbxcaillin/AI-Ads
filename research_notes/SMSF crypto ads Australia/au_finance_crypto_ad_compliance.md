# Australian compliance guardrails for a humorous SMSF investment platform ad (shares, ETFs, crypto)

Research date: 11 October 2026. Australia only. This is not legal advice. Every rule below should be confirmed by a financial services lawyer and the advertiser's AFSL compliance team before production and again before launch. Where a finding came from a search-engine summary rather than a page I opened, it is marked "(search summary)".

## 1. ASIC Regulatory Guide 234: balance, warnings, disclaimers, humour, images, returns, "free", comparisons, audience, video and social

### Takeaway
RG 234 was reissued on 9 June 2026 with no transition period. It now speaks directly to TikTok, Reels, YouTube and AI-generated ads. The core test is the overall impression on an ordinary viewer on first viewing. Risks need prominence comparable to benefits, and the ad's tone must not undermine them. A disclaimer that is barely visible in real time, shown only briefly, run as fast-scrolling text, or pushed behind a link or QR code will not fix a misleading headline. RG 234 contains no section on humour. Comedy is assessed under the tone, images and overall impression rules.

### Cited Findings
**Status and scope (2026 reissue)**
- ASIC issued "RG 234 Advertising financial products and services (including credit)" on 9 June 2026. [ASIC RG 234 page](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/regulatory-guides/rg-234-advertising-financial-products-and-services-including-credit-good-practice-guidance/); [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- ASIC said "there is no applicable transition period following the release of the updated RG 234". It consulted from 27 November 2025 to 22 January 2026 (CS 37). [Bright Law, 16 June 2026](https://www.brightlaw.com.au/asic-updates-guidance-on-advertising-financial-products-and-services-including-credit/); [ASIC feedback summary to CS 37](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/2ixprr2v/rg234-cs-37-summary-of-feedback-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 53 (past performance in promotional material) was withdrawn and folded into RG 234. "Good practice guidance" was dropped from the title, which Hall & Wilcox reads as a firmer regulatory stance. [Hall & Wilcox, 27 Aug 2026](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/asic-tightens-advertising-guidance-what-financial-and-credit-service-providers-need-to-know/)
- RG 234.5 covers "any medium in any form, including mass media, audio, film, video, internet ... social media", and applies "regardless of any specific technologies that may be used, including artificial intelligence (AI)". Its note warns that "hallucinated or biased content by AI tools may increase the risk of misleading or deceptive advertising". [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- Hall & Wilcox's summary of the update says all AI-generated advertising should be reviewed and approved by a human before publication. [Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/asic-tightens-advertising-guidance-what-financial-and-credit-service-providers-need-to-know/)

**Balance of risks and benefits; tone (the humour hook)**
- RG 234.29: risk information "should be clear, and not hidden or difficult to understand, and should be given sufficient prominence to information about returns and benefits. The tone of the advertisement should not undermine the importance of the risks." [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.20: an ad "should not present a one-sided view of a service or product's key features to overstate the benefits". [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.30 to 234.32: safety must not be overstated. Products with high or unusual risk need clear risk disclosure. "When a product or service poses significant risks that would not ordinarily be expected by a consumer, an advertisement should include a prominent warning." [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- Example 19: ASIC objected to "Stress-free strategies to accelerate your wealth" for geared share investing, and the firm stopped using "stress-free". Example 17: "Options trading is easy" plus monthly return claims led to an enforceable undertaking and refunds. Example 18: CFD ads should not say "low-risk trading strategies" or "Safely harness the leverage". [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- A text search of the 2026 RG 234 PDF for "humour" or "humor" returns nothing. ASIC addresses comedy only through tone (RG 234.29), images (RG 234.145 to 234.148) and overall impression (RG 234.196). [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

**Warnings, disclaimers, fine print (including "too small or too fast")**
- RG 234.34: "the more that a qualification is required to balance the information contained in the headline claim, the more prominently placed the qualification should be. The headline claim must not itself be misleading." [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.35: disclaimers need "sufficient prominence to effectively convey key information to a reasonable member of the audience on first viewing". Information is "less likely to be noticed and understood if it is in fine print, contained within a dense block of text, only shown on television or a computer screen for a brief period, or placed where there is distracting content shown simultaneously." [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.36 and 234.173: a link, QR code or PDS reference "will not be sufficient to correct a misleading or deceptive headline claim" (citing ACCC v TPG [2011] FCA 1254). Promoters "should not over rely on 'click through' links or QR codes". [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.37 to 234.40: disclaimers should be "in a form similar to the main body" and in the same language as the ad. A qualification "must be published at the same time as the original message". "Some headline claims are so strong that any separate qualification will not correct any misleading impression." [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.41: warnings that the law requires (as distinct from qualifiers of a headline) need not be as prominent as the headline. Their content or format may still be prescribed. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.166 to 234.168 (film and video, expressly "including short video clips through social media, for example, Instagram reels or TikTok videos"): warnings "should be prominent despite the distractions and time limitations. An average viewer should easily understand any disclaimer or conditions on the first viewing". RG 234.168: "reasonable consumers may not pay attention to disclaimers that are barely visible in real time", citing ASIC v Latitude Finance Australia (No 2) [2024] FCA 1205 at [384]. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.164: audio disclaimers must "be read at a speed that is easy for an average listener to understand". RG 234.178: "lengthy disclaimers that scroll quickly" fail the first-viewing test. Table 2 adds that video risk information should not be "undermined by distracting sounds or images". [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.158 and 234.175: the guidance applies "regardless of format, physical limitations (short form or long form) and time the consumer has to see, hear or interact with the advertisement (e.g. ... stories and reels on Instagram or short clips on TikTok)". If a medium's limits make balance impossible, "the promoter should reconsider if the advertising medium chosen is appropriate". [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.174: ads on third-party platforms such as Facebook and Instagram need special care, because viewers have "less motivation" to click through to extra information. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.177: promoters should keep a record of each ad, including its disclaimers. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

**Images**
- RG 234.145 to 234.148: images can make "a particularly significant impression ... potentially more significant than that created by written or spoken messages". An image that contradicts or reduces the prominence of a qualifier makes an ad more likely to mislead. "The use of imagery associated with success, wealth, safety and security may particularly distract consumers from any contrasting messages about the risks". Images must not misrepresent "the nature, stage or scale of the product or the issuer's business ... whether or not the photograph has a disclaimer." [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.150: if an example shows an upward change, a downward example "should also be included (and vice versa)". [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- Example 69 (finfluencers): ASIC issued warning notices over content using "images of lavish lifestyles, sports cars and luxury goods" alongside claims about trading success. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

**Return and past performance claims**
- RG 234.81 to 234.88: past performance must carry a warning, "unambiguously and without reservation", that it is not indicative of future performance. The warning must be legible or audible in the main part of the ad, close to the figures, on the same page with no click-through, and "not buried". Suggested wording: "Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future performance." Wording ASIC considers "less likely to be effective": "Past performance is not a guarantee of future performance" and "Future returns may vary". [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.82 and 234.101: in broadcast and online media, performance data more than three months past its end date is not "up to date". RG 234.95 and 234.106 cover periods under five years and annualisation. RG 234.109 to 234.119 restrict forecasts and "non-actual" (back-tested or hypothetical) performance. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- Bright Law reports that for performance periods under five years, comparisons should use a period "the same or similar to that of the advertised product". [Bright Law](https://www.brightlaw.com.au/asic-updates-guidance-on-advertising-financial-products-and-services-including-credit/)

**"Free", "secure", "guaranteed" and similar words**
- RG 234.120 to 234.122: take care with "free", "secure" and "guaranteed" where use departs from ordinary meaning. Example 32 covers "free" transactions where costs are still payable. Example 48 covers "free debt assistance" where only the first call was free. Example 50: "Invest with certainty!" led to an enforceable undertaking. Example 51: likening options to "renting real estate" led to an enforceable undertaking. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.44 to 234.49: fees must be presented realistically. Do not claim only one fee if there are others. An advice service must not be called "free" or "low cost" if it is paid for indirectly. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

**Comparisons, ratings, awards, endorsements**
- RG 234.63 to 234.80: compare like with like and be accurate and balanced. State the limits of short-period comparisons. Use only current ratings, explain them and say that ratings are only one factor. Award details should appear in the ad itself. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.128 to 234.132: no false implied endorsement. Take care with third-party logos, which may imply "institutional backing". Never use the ASIC logo without approval (Moneysmart may be referenced). Testimonials must be authentic and attributed. Celebrity or well-known-identity endorsements must be "genuine and truthful" and reflect "reasonably held, informed opinions". [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

**Target audience**
- RG 234.133 and 234.134: consider "the characteristics of the actual audience that is likely to see the advertisement (e.g. their financial literacy, knowledge, demographics and vulnerability)". The actual audience "must be taken into account" even where it differs from the intended one (ASIC v Latitude [2024] FCA 1205). [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.135 to 234.138: statements about who a product suits must be consistent with the TMD. Do not imply suitability for a class unless it has actually been assessed. Products appropriate only for a limited group should be targeted only at that group. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.143 and 234.144: "The more complex the product, the less likely it can be advertised in limited space (e.g. internet banner advertisements ... 30-second television commercials) without being misleading." [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

**Social, sponsored content and publishers**
- RG 234.160 to 234.162 and Example 67: paid content on Instagram or TikTok must be clearly set apart from editorial or opinion content, or carry a "clear and prominent statement that it is advertising content". [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.187: if a promoter learns of a misleading third-party comment on its own Facebook page and leaves it up, it becomes responsible for it (ACCC v Allergy Pathway (No 2) [2011] FCA 74). [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- RG 234.186: an influencer actively promoting a product is treated as being in the promoter's position. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

**Crypto example inside RG 234**
- Example 7 describes a crypto-token payment product whose marketing falsely said the tokens could be exchanged on independent exchanges. The court found the conduct misleading. This matches the Qoin case in section 4. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

**Penalties referenced in RG 234 Table 3**
- The Table 3 provisions include Corporations Act s1041E and s1041F (offences, up to 15 years' imprisonment), s1041H (civil), s769C and ASIC Act s12BB (future representations without reasonable grounds), ASIC Act s12DA (misleading conduct, civil) and s12DB (false or misleading representations: offence, pecuniary penalty, infringement notices, adverse publicity orders). [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Humour is allowed if it does not mislead.** A joke at the expense of the old way of doing things is lower risk. A joke at the expense of risk itself (crypto "to the moon", "can't lose", "easy money", a mascot shrugging off volatility) is high risk under RG 234.29 and the RG 234.121 term list. The punchline must not carry an implied claim about returns, safety or ease.
- **Video disclaimer craft (practical reading of RG 234.35 and 234.166 to 234.168, not a numeric ASIC rule):** ASIC gives no minimum seconds or font size. To meet the first-viewing test, put the risk line on screen for long enough to read at normal speed, in a type size and contrast similar to the main supers, without competing animation or gags in the same frame. Voice it if the headline is voiced (RG 234.37 "form similar to the main body"). Do not rely on an end-card link or QR code.
- **Crypto volatility, the possibility of losing the whole investment, and crypto's different regulatory status on a mixed platform** are risks "that would not ordinarily be expected" by many viewers. Read with RG 234.32 and 234.33, they point to a prominent risk warning inside the ad itself.
- **Short formats:** a 6 to 15 second TikTok or Reel that names crypto and implies upside may be impossible to balance. RG 234.175 then says to change the medium. Brand-only or awareness cuts (no product claims, no returns) are the safer short-form option.
- **AI-generated footage:** human sign-off is expected. No generated imagery should imply real wealth, an ASIC or ATO endorsement, a real exchange or a real person's testimonial.

### Gaps
- RG 234 gives no numeric standard (seconds on screen, minimum font size or words per minute). The Latitude judgment [2024] FCA 1205 at [384] is the closest authority, and I did not read it directly.
- I found no ASIC statement or enforcement example that addresses humour as such.

## 2. General advice warnings, when an ad becomes "financial product advice", AFSL requirements and showing licence numbers

### Takeaway
An ad that recommends, or could reasonably be seen as intended to influence, a decision about a financial product (for example "move your SMSF into ETFs and crypto with us") is likely general advice. That brings in licensing and the s949A general advice warning. An ad for a product that needs a PDS must say a PDS is available and should be considered, and must describe the target market or say where the TMD is (s1018A). No rule found requires an AFSL number on every financial product ad. A credit licence number is required in printed credit ads, and Meta requires the AFSL number (or an exemption) in its verified "Paid for by" label.

### Cited Findings
- INFO 269: advice is a recommendation or opinion "intended to influence, or reasonably seen as intended to influence" a financial decision. Describing features without recommending is generally not advice. Presenting facts that imply someone should invest can be advice. Being paid makes it more likely the content is advice. [ASIC INFO 269](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services/giving-financial-product-advice/discussing-financial-products-and-services-online/)
- INFO 269: carrying on a financial services business requires an AFSL unless exempt or acting as an authorised representative of a licensee. Penalties include imprisonment for individuals. [ASIC INFO 269](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services/giving-financial-product-advice/discussing-financial-products-and-services-online/)
- General advice to retail clients requires a general advice warning under s949A. The warning covers that the advice was prepared without considering the client's objectives, financial situation or needs, and that the client should consider the PDS before acting. It must be given at the same time and by the same means as the advice. [ASIC RG 244](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/tkqi11il/rg244-published-13-december-2012-20211208.pdf); [ASIC, limited AFS licensees advice obligations](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-finance-professionals/afs-licensees/limited-afs-licensees/limited-afs-licensees-advice-conduct-and-disclosure-obligations) (search summary)
- s1018A: advertising or promotional material for a financial product offered to retail clients that requires a PDS must say that a PDS is or will be available, where and when it is available, and that the person should consider it before acquiring the product. Where a TMD is required, the material must describe the target market or say where the TMD can be found. Breach is an offence. [AustLII s1018A](https://www5.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca2001172/s1018a.html); [RG 274.138](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/etgm1amc/rg274-published-10-september-2024.pdf)
- RG 234.41, note 2: a credit licensee must include its credit licence number in any printed advertisement (National Credit Act s52; reg 13). [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- Meta's Australian financial services advertiser verification (announced December 2024, phased in by February 2025) requires businesses to give their AFSL number or declare an exemption. Verified ads show a "Paid for by" label with the licence number or exemption. [Mediaweek](https://mediaweek.com.au/meta-rolls-out-verification-requirements-for-financial-ads); [Mi-3](https://www.mi-3.com.au/02-12-2024/meta-tightens-australian-verification-measures-financial-ads)
- RG 234.151 and 234.153: advice-service ads must not create unrealistic expectations. Digital or AI-enabled advice tools must not be overstated, and their risks and limitations need "sufficient prominence". [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Practical end-card pattern:** issuer name with "AFSL [number]" (or "Authorised Representative [number] of [licensee] AFSL [number]"). Add a general advice line ("General information only. It does not take into account your objectives, financial situation or needs."). If any advertised product needs a PDS, add "Consider the PDS and TMD at [url] before deciding." If performance is shown, add the past-performance line. Showing the licence number is market practice and needed for Meta verification. A strict statutory duty to show it in every non-credit ad was not confirmed (see Gaps).
- **Platform-level PDS question:** whether the platform is offered under a PDS (for example an IDPS or managed account service) or is a broker giving access to listed shares decides whether s1018A bites. That needs legal review.
- **SMSF framing:** a line like "Your SMSF deserves better" is lower risk than "You should set up an SMSF to buy crypto". The second looks like a recommendation about establishing an SMSF and about a product class (see section 5).

### Gaps
- I could not open Corporations Act s912F (obligation to cite licence number in prescribed documents) to confirm whether it reaches advertising. AustLII returned 403 and search found nothing. Legal review is needed.
- I did not confirm the exact current text of ASIC Corporations instruments on oral general advice warnings for audio or video ads.

## 3. Design and distribution obligations (DDO): TMDs, stop orders and what DDO means for advertising

### Takeaway
DDO (in force since 5 October 2021) treats marketing as distribution. ASIC checks whether promotional campaigns direct a product to its target market, and RG 274 says mass-market advertising, including banner ads, is generally not appropriate for a product with a narrow target market. Penalties so far: $8m (Amex, 2024), $8m (Firstmac, for cross-marketing outside the TMD, 2024) and $8m (Bit Trade/Kraken, crypto margin product with no TMD, December 2024). ASIC had made 97 interim and 2 final stop orders by July 2026. I found no stop order expressly about a social media campaign. Most fully paid ordinary shares are outside DDO. ETFs and other PDS products are inside it.

### Cited Findings
- RG 274 coverage: excluded products include MySuper products, margin lending facilities and "most ordinary shares". Products needing a PDS are covered. [RG 274 (Sept 2024)](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/etgm1amc/rg274-published-10-september-2024.pdf)
- RG 274 Table 4 (issuer's reasonable steps): "We will take into account whether an issuer's promotional materials and marketing campaigns direct distribution towards the target market." [RG 274](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/etgm1amc/rg274-published-10-september-2024.pdf)
- RG 274 Table 6 (distributor's reasonable steps): ASIC will consider "the content and medium of delivery" of ads. "Mass market advertising or prominent online methods, such as banner advertising, generally are not appropriate for a product with a narrow target market." [RG 274](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/etgm1amc/rg274-published-10-september-2024.pdf)
- RG 274.99: distribution conditions for narrow-market products may include "targeted advertising". [RG 274](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/etgm1amc/rg274-published-10-september-2024.pdf)
- RG 274.261 to 274.263 (exchange-traded products): an ETP issuer is not responsible for every on-market trade. If it is "actively marketing its product through a broker or other distribution channel", it should take reasonable steps to keep that marketing consistent with the TMD. [RG 274](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/etgm1amc/rg274-published-10-september-2024.pdf)
- RG 234.135: statements about who a product suits must be consistent with the TMD. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- INFO 269: licensees using influencers must "take reasonable steps to ensure promotion reaches only the target market" for DDO products. [ASIC INFO 269](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services/giving-financial-product-advice/discussing-financial-products-and-services-online/)
- Firstmac (Federal Court, 2024): Firstmac breached s994E(3) by cross-marketing its High Livez fund by email and phone to term deposit holders, some likely outside the TMD. The court described the conduct as "objectively reckless" and imposed an $8m penalty (ASIC sought $25m). [Broker Daily](https://www.brokerdaily.au/regulation/19883-non-bank-fails-to-meet-ddo-obligations-ordered-to-pay-8m) (search summary); [ASIC 22-361MR](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2022-releases/22-361mr-asic-takes-further-civil-penalty-action-for-breaches-of-design-and-distribution-obligations/)
- American Express ([2024] FCA 784, July 2024): $8m, the first DDO monetary penalty, for failing to review a TMD. [Ashurst](https://ashurst.com/en/insights/asic-v-amex-court-orders-first-penalty-of-8-million-dollars) (search summary)
- Bit Trade (Kraken): on 23 August 2024 the Federal Court found Bit Trade issued a margin extension crypto credit product to more than 1,100 customers without a TMD. Penalty $8m plus costs (December 2024). Customers paid over US$7m in fees and interest and lost over US$5m. ASIC Chair Longo: "Target market determinations are fundamental in ensuring that investors are not inappropriately marketed". [ASIC 24-274MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2024-releases/24-274mr-kraken-crypto-exchange-operator-to-pay-8-million-following-asic-enforcement-action/)
- DDO stop orders: ASIC issued interim stop orders against two Stratfund products in July 2026, taking its totals to 97 interim and 2 final DDO stop orders. [ASIC 26-142MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-142mr-asic-issues-ddo-stop-orders-against-stratfund-s-australian-fixed-income-fund/) (search summary). 2025 examples: La Trobe US Private Credit Fund (lifted after the TMD cut the appropriate share of investable assets from 35% to 10%) and City Finance Lending (distribution conditions). [ASIC 25-205MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2025-releases/25-205mr-asic-issues-ddo-stop-order-against-la-trobe-us-private-credit-fund/); [ASIC 25-284MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2025-releases/25-284mr-asic-issues-ddo-stop-order-against-city-finance-lending-pty-ltd)
- RG 234.185: publishers are expected to stop running an ad once told it is subject to a stop order. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Targeting the ad:** the platform's own TMD (and the TMDs of any ETFs it actively promotes) should drive targeting. Use 18+ (also a platform rule), interest and lookalike audiences close to SMSF trustees and accountants, and exclude very young or broad entertainment audiences where the TMD is narrow. Comedy spreads widely, and RG 234.134 says the actual audience is what counts.
- Under DDO, crypto that is not a financial product sits outside TMD rules. Crypto products that are financial products (yield, margin, wrapped tokens, some stablecoins) are inside. The Kraken case shows a crypto business can be penalised for skipping a TMD.
- A cross-sell message ("Already holding crypto? Put it in your SMSF") sent to an existing customer base is the pattern ASIC litigated in Firstmac.

### Gaps
- I found no DDO stop order or case expressly about a social media or influencer campaign. Enforcement has focused on TMD content and distribution conditions.
- I did not confirm whether RG 274 has been updated after September 2024.

## 4. ASIC enforcement on misleading crypto and investment marketing; finfluencers (INFO 269); 2024 to 2026 updates

### Takeaway
ASIC's crypto cases are mostly about licensing, with misleading-marketing findings attached (Qoin: $12m of a $14m penalty was for misleading conduct, 2026). The June 2026 High Court Block Earner decision widens what counts as a financial product. ASIC lost Finder Wallet on appeal in July 2025. On finfluencers, ASIC used INFO 269 (March 2022) and in April 2026 turned to licensees: four warning notices, plus a review of three licensees supervising 15 finfluencer authorised representatives.

### Cited Findings
- **Qoin (ASIC v BPS Financial):** penalty judgment [2026] FCA 18, total $14m ($2m unlicensed conduct, $12m misleading and deceptive conduct). Orders bar false statements about the number of wallet holders, whether tokens can be exchanged for fiat or other crypto, and whether the wallet was officially approved or registered. Adverse publicity notices are required on the app and website. ASIC: crypto products "can be highly volatile, inherently risky and complex" and "the digital asset industry is well on notice". [ASIC 26-008MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-008mr-bps-financial-to-pay-14-million-in-penalties-over-crypto-qoin-wallet/); [Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/asic-v-bps-financial-qoin-federal-court-orders-14-million-penalty-in-landmark-crypto-case/). The Full Federal Court had allowed ASIC's appeal on the licensing point on 30 May 2025 (search summary).
- **Block Earner (ASIC v Web3 Ventures):** at first instance (2024) the Earner crypto-yield product needed an AFSL, but no penalty was imposed. [ASIC 24-019MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2024-releases/24-019mr-court-finds-block-earner-needed-financial-services-licence-to-offer-earner-crypto-product). The Full Court reversed on 22 April 2025 (search summary). On 17 June 2026 the High Court unanimously allowed ASIC's appeal ([2026] HCA 21): the Earner product was a financial product (a facility for making a financial investment, and a derivative). The penalty question was remitted to the Full Federal Court. [Piper Alderman via Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/blockchain-bites-high-court-endorses-expansive-financial-product-definitions-in-block-earner-appeal%3B-asic-hits-pause-no-action-relief-for-digital-asset-businesses-extended-to-30-september-2026%3B-ready-or-not-australias-travel-rule-starts-on-1-july/1814008); [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/high-court-rules-crypto-yield-products-require-an-afsl). Piper Alderman notes "crypto product labels will carry little weight", so marketing names like "Earn" or "Lend" do not decide regulatory status.
- **Finder Wallet (Finder Earn):** ASIC sued in 2022 alleging unlicensed conduct and inadequate risk disclosure. [ASIC 22-359MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2022-releases/22-359mr-finder-wallet-sued-for-alleged-unlicensed-conduct-and-inadequate-risk-disclosure-over-finder-earn-product/). ASIC lost at first instance in March 2024. The Full Court dismissed its appeal on 24 July 2025 ([2025] FCAFC 93), holding that the product was not a debenture. [ASIC 25-147MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2025-releases/25-147mr-asic-s-appeal-against-finder-wallet-decision-dismissed/) (search summary). One secondary source reports ASIC sought High Court special leave; this is unconfirmed.
- **Bit Trade (Kraken):** $8m DDO penalty, December 2024 (see section 3). [ASIC 24-274MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2024-releases/24-274mr-kraken-crypto-exchange-operator-to-pay-8-million-following-asic-enforcement-action/)
- **INFO 269 (March 2022):** misleading-conduct rules apply even without a licence, and intent is not required. Predictions need reasonable grounds. Risks need similar prominence to benefits. "Risk-free" claims are likely misleading. A paid unique link earning per click-through is likely "dealing by arranging". Licensees may be liable for influencers and must supervise them. [ASIC INFO 269](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services/giving-financial-product-advice/discussing-financial-products-and-services-online/)
- **April 2026 finfluencer action (26-081MR, 24 April 2026):** ASIC issued warning notices to four finfluencers (suspected unlicensed advice or misleading conduct, including "guaranteed returns"). It reviewed three licensees supervising 15 finfluencer authorised representatives as part of a global week of action. Commissioner Kirkland: "We expect active supervision, not a set-and-forget approach." [ASIC 26-081MR](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-081mr-asic-continues-finfluencer-crackdown-alongside-global-regulators/); [Professional Planner](https://www.professionalplanner.com.au/2026/04/asic-steps-up-finfluencer-crackdown-as-it-targets-licensees/) (search summary)
- RG 234 (2026) says finfluencers "must ensure that promotional material is not misleading and complies with other relevant financial services laws" (RG 234.179, Example 69). [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Content to avoid**, drawn from the cases: inflated user counts, implied regulator approval ("ASIC registered", "government backed"), claims that tokens can be freely exchanged, "earn" or "yield" language for crypto, and influencer cuts with paid links that are not supervised under the AFSL.
- If the ad uses creators or comedians, treat them under INFO 269. Sign a documented agreement, script and pre-approve content, show the paid partnership label, avoid per-click remuneration unless the creator is an authorised representative, and monitor comments (RG 234.187).

### Gaps
- I did not find the Qoin judgment's list of specific ad channels.
- Whether ASIC obtained High Court special leave in Finder Wallet is unconfirmed.
- The outcome of the Block Earner penalty remittal is unknown as at October 2026.

## 5. SMSF-specific: ASIC and ATO warnings, recommending an SMSF, ASIC reports on SMSF advice and promotion

### Takeaway
ASIC has explicitly warned about marketing that pushes people from APRA funds into SMSFs to buy crypto, and tells consumers not to rely on social media ads. Its November 2025 review of SMSF establishment advice (REP 824) found 62 of 100 files failed the best interests duty. Since February 2026 it has been pursuing lead generators in super switching. The ATO allows SMSF crypto only within the trust deed, the investment strategy, the sole purpose test, separate fund wallets and the related-party rules. An ad that nudges viewers to set up or switch to an SMSF for crypto is in ASIC's highest-concern zone.

### Cited Findings
- ASIC warning: ASIC "has noticed an increase in marketing recommending Australians switch from retail and industry superannuation funds to SMSFs". SMSF trustees are being targeted to buy crypto-assets, crypto may breach an SMSF's trust deed, and consumers should not "rely on social media ads or online contact from someone promoting an 'investment opportunity'". The trustee "ultimately bear[s] responsibility for the fund's decisions". [ASIC warning: SMSFs and crypto](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/articles/warning-self-managed-super-funds-and-crypto-investments/) (search summary; page date appears to be about 2022)
- ASIC REP 824 (6 November 2025), Review of SMSF establishment advice: 100 files reviewed. 62 failed to show compliance with the best interests duty and 27 raised significant concerns about client detriment. It sets 8 action points for advisers and 4 for licensees, including not mis-selling SMSFs on the basis of "control". ASIC is considering enforcement. [ASIC REP 824](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/reports/rep-824-review-of-smsf-establishment-advice/); [ASIC 25-265MR](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2025-releases/25-265mr-asic-review-raises-fresh-concerns-over-risks-to-retirement-savings-from-poor-smsf-advice/) (search summary)
- February 2026: ASIC began reviewing advice licensees that use lead generators after the Shield and First Guardian collapses, and published a list of lead-generation entities. Its red flags include pressure to act now, claims your fund is underperforming, and free "super health checks". [ABC News, 18 Feb 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-02-18/asic-announces-review-into-lead-generators-superannuation/106353740) (search summary)
- RG 234 (2026) now names lead generators as promoters. ASIC's feedback summary says it is doing "significant work in high-risk superannuation switching", and future RG 234 updates may cover that. [Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/asic-tightens-advertising-guidance-what-financial-and-credit-service-providers-need-to-know/); [ASIC CS 37 feedback summary](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/2ixprr2v/rg234-cs-37-summary-of-feedback-published-09-june-2026.pdf)
- 2016 precedent: ASIC's SMSF taskforce made three firms remove or amend misleading SMSF social media ads. [ASIC 16-041MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2016-releases/16-041mr-asic-stops-potentially-misleading-smsf-social-media-advertising)
- From 1 July 2026, advertising super funds during employee onboarding is banned, with exemptions for some MySuper, employer default and stapled funds. ASIC is taking a "balanced approach" for 12 months. [Bright Law](https://www.brightlaw.com.au/asic-updates-guidance-on-advertising-financial-products-and-services-including-credit/)
- ATO: SMSFs may hold crypto only if the trust deed allows it and the investment strategy covers it, under SIS Act and SIS Regulations rules. Crypto must be held in the fund's own wallet, separate from members'. It cannot be acquired from members or related parties. Personal benefits to trustees (such as affiliate fees) risk the sole purpose test. Valuations must be at market value from a reputable exchange, and records must be kept. [ATO: SMSF investing in crypto assets](https://ato.gov.au/Super/Self-managed-super-funds/In-detail/SMSF-investing/SMSF-investing-in-crypto-assets) (search summary; page last updated about June 2022)

### Inferences
- **Don'ts for the script:** do not tell viewers to switch from their industry or retail fund, set up an SMSF, or "take control" of super to buy crypto. Do not run "free super health check" style hooks. Do not mock APRA funds as underperforming without substantiation. Do not imply SMSF crypto is simple or ATO-approved.
- **Safer framing:** speak to existing SMSF trustees and their accountants about the workflow (one platform for shares, ETFs and crypto, with fund-name accounts and records for the auditor). Use compliance pain points as the comedy (paperwork, separate wallets, audit trail) rather than returns. That fits ATO separation and record-keeping expectations, if the product really delivers those features.
- If the platform pays per lead or uses affiliates, those parties are "promoters" under RG 234 (2026) and are in ASIC's lead-generator focus.

### Gaps
- I did not find a 2025 or 2026 ASIC or ATO publication specifically on SMSF crypto advertising. The core ASIC warning page appears to date from around 2022.
- I did not find a source for the current licensing rules on recommending that someone establish an SMSF (for example the end of the accountants' exemption). This needs legal review.

## 6. Crypto-specific: is crypto a financial product; digital asset platform legislation status; mixed platforms

### Takeaway
Bitcoin itself is unlikely to be a financial product (ASIC INFO 225). Yield, margin, wrapped-token and many stablecoin arrangements can be. The Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026 (passed 1 April 2026, assent 8 April 2026) makes operators of digital asset platforms and tokenised custody platforms need an AFSL. The new categories apply from about 9 April 2027, with an 18-month window from assent to apply. Until then, misleading-crypto-marketing exposure is real (Qoin), and a mixed platform must avoid implying its crypto side carries the same protections as its regulated shares and ETF side.

### Cited Findings
- The Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Act 2026 (Act No. 38 of 2026) passed both Houses on 1 April 2026 and received assent on 8 April 2026. It brings digital asset platforms and tokenised custody platforms into AFS licensing. The bill was introduced on 26 November 2025 after a September 2025 exposure draft. [Federal Register of Legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00038/asmade/text); [APH bill page](https://www.aph.gov.au/Parliamentary_Business/Bills_Legislation/Bills_Search_Results/Result?bId=r7411); [Treasury consultation](https://consult.treasury.gov.au/c2025-701519)
- Commencement is 12 months after assent, and operators have 18 months from assent to apply for an AFSL. Existing operators who apply in the window may keep operating while ASIC assesses. Low-value exemption: no more than $10m in transactions over 12 months and under $5,000 per customer. Whether a token is itself a financial product remains a token-by-token question. [Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/digital-assets-reform-passes-18-month-licensing-window-opens-for-platform-operators/)
- Piper Alderman gives 9 April 2027 as the date the new DAP and TCP categories apply. [Piper Alderman via Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/blockchain-bites-high-court-endorses-expansive-financial-product-definitions-in-block-earner-appeal%3B-asic-hits-pause-no-action-relief-for-digital-asset-businesses-extended-to-30-september-2026%3B-ready-or-not-australias-travel-rule-starts-on-1-july/1814008)
- ASIC INFO 225 (updated late October or November 2025): Bitcoin is unlikely to be a financial product. Fiat-backed stablecoins are treated as non-cash payment facilities. Yield-bearing stablecoins are likely managed investment schemes. Wrapped tokens are treated as derivatives (untested). [Gilbert + Tobin](https://www.gtlaw.com.au/insights/key-topics/regulation-in-motion/information-sheet-225-info-225); [ASIC news item on exemptions](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/news-items/asic-finalises-new-exemptions-to-support-digital-asset-innovation/) (search summary; sources disagree on the exact INFO 225 date)
- ASIC's sector-wide no-action position for unlicensed digital asset businesses that apply for an AFSL was extended on 25 June 2026 from 30 June 2026 to 30 September 2026. [Piper Alderman via Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/blockchain-bites-high-court-endorses-expansive-financial-product-definitions-in-block-earner-appeal%3B-asic-hits-pause-no-action-relief-for-digital-asset-businesses-extended-to-30-september-2026%3B-ready-or-not-australias-travel-rule-starts-on-1-july/1814008)
- AUSTRAC: the travel rule started 1 July 2026, and newly regulated virtual asset service providers had to enrol and register by 29 July 2026. [Piper Alderman via Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/blockchain-bites-high-court-endorses-expansive-financial-product-definitions-in-block-earner-appeal%3B-asic-hits-pause-no-action-relief-for-digital-asset-businesses-extended-to-30-september-2026%3B-ready-or-not-australias-travel-rule-starts-on-1-july/1814008)
- High Court (17 June 2026) took a broad approach to "financial product" in Block Earner (section 4). [MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/high-court-rules-crypto-yield-products-require-an-afsl)

### Inferences
- **Mixed platform ad rules:**
  - Do not let the regulated shares and ETF side "lend" protections to crypto. Avoid "ASIC regulated platform" or "licensed" claims that imply the crypto holdings are covered, unless legal review confirms they are. RG 234.128 bars implied endorsement, and Qoin was penalised for implying official approval.
  - Disclose plainly that crypto is high risk and may not be a regulated financial product.
  - Any "earn" or "staking yield" feature is likely a financial product after Block Earner.
- **Status as of 11 October 2026:** the no-action relief deadline (30 September 2026) has passed, and the DAF regime starts in April 2027. Confirm the platform's AFSL and AUSTRAC status and its crypto custody arrangement before any claim about it appears on screen.
- When crypto is not a financial product, misleading-crypto-ad claims may fall under the Australian Consumer Law (ACCC) rather than the ASIC Act. I found no source confirming how that split applies to a mixed platform. Legal review is needed.

### Gaps
- I did not read the Act text to confirm the commencement and window dates. They come from law firm summaries, and Piper Alderman refers to it inconsistently as both a Bill and an Act.
- I could not confirm whether ASIC extended the no-action relief beyond 30 September 2026.

## 7. Advertising Standards (AANA codes, Ad Standards complaints) relevant to finance humour

### Takeaway
The AANA Code of Ethics (in force from 1 February 2021, under review since October 2025) adds community-standards limits. Its Practice Note says humour does not excuse a negative depiction of a group with a protected attribute (age is one, which matters for older SMSF trustees). Language, safety and distinguishability rules (for example #ad on influencer posts) also apply. Misleading financial claims are mainly for ASIC and the courts, but Section 1 of the Code also covers legality and misleading content.

### Cited Findings
- AANA Code of Ethics, effective 1 February 2021. Section 1 (competitor complaints, Industry Jury) covers legality and misleading or deceptive ads. Section 2 (consumer complaints, Community Panel) covers 2.1 discrimination and vilification (including age), 2.2 exploitative sexual appeal, 2.3 violence, 2.4 sex and nudity, 2.5 language (avoid strong or obscene language), 2.6 health and safety, and 2.7 distinguishability (influencer posts need labels such as "#ad"). [AANA Code of Ethics](https://aana.com.au/self-regulation/code-of-ethics/)
- The Practice Note says humour or satire does not excuse a negative overall depiction of people with a protected attribute. [AANA Code of Ethics page and Practice Note](https://aana.com.au/self-regulation/code-of-ethics/); [Practice Note PDF](https://aana.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/AANA_Code_of_Ethics_PracticeNote_Effective_February_2021.pdf)
- AANA ran a public review of the Code (discussion paper, October 2025). The paper notes 307 complaints in 2024 about misleading ads that fell outside the Code's scope. [AANA discussion paper Oct 2025](https://info.aana.com.au/hubfs/Self-Regulation/20251007%20Code%20of%20Ethics%20Review%20-%20Discussion%20Paper/AANA%20Code%20of%20Ethics%20Review%202025%20Discussion%20Paper.pdf) (search summary)
- RG 234 itself cites the AANA Code's requirement that advertising be clearly distinguishable as advertising. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Comedy guardrails:** avoid jokes that make older people, retirees or "boomers" look foolish (2.1, age), skip swearing for laughs (2.5), and avoid stunts showing unsafe behaviour (2.6). An SMSF audience skews older, and a confused-retiree gag is a likely complaint trigger.
- Ad Standards cannot fine anyone, but adverse determinations are public and usually lead to the ad being pulled.

### Gaps
- I found no Ad Standards case report on a humorous crypto or investment ad. A direct search of the Ad Standards case database is needed.
- The outcome of the 2025 to 2026 AANA Code review (any new code or effective date) is not confirmed.

## 8. Platform policies in Australia (Meta, Google and YouTube, TikTok) and any SMSF or super ad requirements

### Takeaway
These rules decide whether the ad can run at all:
- **Google and YouTube:** financial services verification has been required in Australia since August 2022. Australia is not on Google's approved list for crypto exchange or software wallet ads, so a creative or landing page that promotes buying crypto cannot run there.
- **Meta:** needs written permission to advertise a crypto exchange or trading platform, using AUSTRAC registration or an ASIC AFSL or Australian Markets Licence. All financial services advertisers must also be verified with an AFSL number or an exemption.
- **TikTok (Sept 2026):** allows licensed investment services to users aged 18 and over. It allows crypto exchange services only with TikTok permission via a sales rep, licensing and 18+ targeting. It bans ads for virtual currencies, crypto wallets and crypto advisory services. It bans financial products in creator branded content.

### Cited Findings
**Google and YouTube**
- Google's cryptocurrency policy lists approved locations for exchange and software wallet ads (EU and EEA under MiCA, Switzerland, the UK, the US, Japan, Canada, Hong Kong and others). Australia is not listed. If a targeted location "is not on this list", these products "can't be advertised". Certification moves into the Google Ads interface from June 2026. Prohibited everywhere: ICOs, DeFi trading protocols, crypto loans, unhosted software wallets, trading signals and investment advice, and affiliate or aggregator sites. [Google Ads: Cryptocurrencies and related products](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/14009787?hl=en)
- Google requires Australian financial services advertisers to be verified, including showing ASIC licensing, with G2RS third-party verification. Enforced from 30 August 2022. [Google financial services verification: regulators and dates](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/12390454); [Google Australia blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-au/australian-financial-services-advertisers-verification/) (search summary)
- RG 234.170 and 234.176 name YouTube and search ads as advertising within RG 234. [RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)

**Meta (Facebook, Instagram)**
- Written permission is needed for ads promoting crypto exchanges or trading platforms, crypto lending, wallets that buy, sell, swap or stake, and crypto investment solicitation (including affiliates and aggregators). Advertisers apply through Business Suite, Authorizations and Verifications. Accepted Australian credentials: AUSTRAC registration, or an ASIC Australian Financial Services Licence or Australian Markets Licence. Education and news that do not offer crypto products are exempt. Change log entries run to 15 April 2026. [Meta: Cryptocurrency products and services](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/cryptocurrency-products-and-services/)
- Meta's Australian financial services verification covers insurance, loans, investment opportunities, credit cards and apps. Businesses give their AFSL number or declare an exemption, and ads show a "Paid for by" label. Announced 2 December 2024, in force by February 2025, under the Australian Online Scams Code. [Mediaweek](https://mediaweek.com.au/meta-rolls-out-verification-requirements-for-financial-ads); [Mi-3](https://www.mi-3.com.au/02-12-2024/meta-tightens-australian-verification-measures-financial-ads)

**TikTok**
- Financial Services policy, last updated September 2026. Globally: comply with local law, be licensed, include disclaimers, target 18+ only. No get-rich-quick offers or "too-good-to-be-true" financial offers. Australia: investment services may be allowed, and TikTok may ask for evidence of licensing or accreditation. Crypto exchange services may be allowed only if licensed, with permission through a TikTok sales representative, and 18+. Not allowed: ICOs and token sales, binary options, and "Virtual currencies or cryptocurrencies (such as Bitcoin and Ethereum), including cryptocurrency credit or debit cards, wallets, advisory services, and NFT trading or investments". [TikTok Ads: Financial Services](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-financial-services)
- TikTok's branded content policy (2021) bars creators from promoting financial products and services, including crypto, in sponsored content. [Mumbrella](https://mumbrella.com.au/tiktok-prohibits-financial-services-and-products-branded-content-693178)

**SMSF or super specific platform rules**
- No platform policy specific to SMSF or super ads was found. The onboarding advertising ban from 1 July 2026 is a legal rule about employee onboarding, not a platform rule. [Bright Law](https://www.brightlaw.com.au/asic-updates-guidance-on-advertising-financial-products-and-services-including-credit/)

### Inferences
- **Channel plan implied by the policies:**
  - **YouTube and Google:** run a shares and ETF version with no crypto visuals, words or crypto landing page, under verified financial-services status. Whether a mixed platform's homepage triggers the crypto policy is a platform review risk to check with Google.
  - **Meta:** a version that mentions crypto needs Meta written permission plus financial verification.
  - **TikTok:** a paid ad for a platform that offers crypto needs the crypto-exchange permission path. No creator-led branded content about the platform.
- **Plan two cuts:** a crypto-inclusive cut for Meta (once approved) and TikTok (once approved), and a shares and ETF cut for Google and YouTube.
- Every platform requires 18+ targeting or verification. Platform approval does not mean ASIC compliance, and the reverse is also true.

- I could not open Google's Australia financial services verification page directly. Details are from search summaries of Google's pages.
- Third-party blogs claim a March 2026 Meta "three-tier" crypto authorisation system. The official page does not show it, so it is unverified.
- I did not check YouTube-specific creator rules (paid product placement disclosures) for Australian finance content.
