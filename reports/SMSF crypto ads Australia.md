# Turn SMSF crypto paperwork into compliant comedy

Buying crypto inside an Australian self-managed super fund is legal but full of procedure. The trust deed has to allow it and the investment strategy has to cover it. The fund has to own the coins in an exchange account or wallet in its own name, kept strictly apart from the trustee's personal crypto. The fund cannot acquire coins the members already own. It has to value its holdings at market every 30 June, and an auditor has to verify all of this every year ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c); [ATO, Oct 2025](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/auditing-smsfs-with-crypto-assets)). That friction is the comic material for LibertyStone. Australian funny finance work has succeeded when the joke lands on admin, procrastination or the brand itself, and has failed (or been banned) when crypto became the hero that rescues people from hardship or missing out ([AdNews](https://adnews.com.au/news/how-australian-retirement-trust-s-big-blue-monster-led-to-a-business-boom); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/356156/uk-regulator-bans-coinbase-ads-over-cost-of-living-messaging)). The guardrails are tight. ASIC's RG 234, reissued on 9 June 2026 with no transition period, says the tone of an ad must not undermine its risk information, and it now covers AI-generated ads and TikTok-length video ([ASIC RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)). ASIC has also specifically warned about marketing that pushes Australians into SMSFs to buy crypto ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/articles/warning-self-managed-super-funds-and-crypto-investments)). Google does not allow crypto exchange ads in Australia at all ([Google Ads](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/14009787?hl=en)). Coinbase launched an SMSF product on 3 May 2026 with a "fewer logins, one place" pitch ([Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/blog/coinbase-launches-self-managed-super-funds-support-in-australia); [SMSF Adviser](https://www.smsfadviser.com/coinbase-enters-smsf-sector-with-new-solution/)), so LibertyStone has to find its own joke. The strongest open territory is the rules themselves: the deed, the bouncer at the related-party door, the show-and-tell for the auditor and the 30 June scramble. Each concept below is built on a cited rule, and each ends on a plain risk line that the comedy does not touch.

A note on scope. This report assumes LibertyStone offers fund-name accounts, shares, ETFs and crypto on one platform, and records an accountant can use. None of those features was verified in the research, and each one must be substantiated before it appears in an ad.

## Owning SMSF crypto takes a deed, a strategy, a fund-name account and an auditor's sign-off

The ATO's standing guidance (dated 29 June 2022 and read from the ATO's content API because the usual page returned 404 in October 2026) says SMSFs are "not prohibited" from investing in crypto. The investment must, however, "be allowed under the fund's trust deed", follow the investment strategy and meet the same SIS Act and SIS Regulations rules as any other asset. The ATO "strongly encourage[s]" professional advice first ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c)). Each requirement turns into a practical step. **The deed is the first trap.** Older deeds can predate crypto, and one guide warns that deeds which refer only to "shares, property, and cash" can block an exchange account. That warning comes from search summaries of [Kalkine Media](https://kalkinemedia.com/au/stocks/smsf/how-does-an-smsf-open-a-crypto-exchange-account) and [Grow SMSF](https://growsmsf.com.au/cryptocurrency-smsf/), not pages that were opened, while [Finder](https://www.finder.com.au/crypto-self-managed-super-funds) makes the broader point. **The strategy is the second.** ASIC says trustees must document how investments meet retirement goals "having regard to diversification ... liquidity" and "must also be able to demonstrate that the fund owns the asset" ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/articles/warning-self-managed-super-funds-and-crypto-investments)). One SMSF provider adds that a strategy that "gestures at 'other investments' will not carry a volatile allocation through an audit" ([SMSFcentral](https://www.smsfcentral.com.au/smsf-cryptocurrency/)).

**Separation is the third, and it is absolute.** Under Reg 4.09A the fund must keep "a separate crypto wallet for the SMSF from that used by trustees and members personally" ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c)). The ATO's May 2025 newsroom item says to make sure the wallet "is registered in the name of your SMSF" and warns that mixing "can be a breach" of the SIS Act ([ATO, 27 May 2025](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/navigating-smsf-crypto-assets)). **The related-party rule is the fourth and the most counterintuitive.** "Crypto assets are not 'listed securities'", so they "cannot be acquired from a related party". Trustees therefore cannot make in-specie contributions of coins they already own, and the fund has to buy fresh with fund money ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c)). The sole purpose test rules out personal use. On top of that, every swap is a CGT disposal and the fund needs full records, valued at the 30 June closing price from an exchange that publishes historical data ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/api/public/content/0-78a1e793-97f4-4ab9-9f27-9f8e849cd76c); [SMSFcentral](https://www.smsfcentral.com.au/smsf-cryptocurrency/)).

**Onboarding is identity checks stacked several times over.** A typical exchange wants the trust deed, the fund's ABN, the corporate trustee's ACN and registered address, ID for every member and director, a bank account in the fund's name and an investment strategy that covers crypto ([cryptosmsf.com.au, 8 Mar 2026](https://www.cryptosmsf.com.au/guides/best-crypto-exchanges-for-smsf/)). On Swyftx, the trustee reaches "Gold-level" personal KYC first and then opens the fund account (same source). Beneficiaries holding more than 25% need their own verified accounts. That detail comes from search snippets of [Fullstack](https://www.fullstack.com.au/?p=10501) and [Swyftx](https://swyftx.com.au/smsf-investing-in-cryptocurrencies/), whose live SMSF page rendered empty when fetched in October 2026. Around eight to ten Australian exchanges accept SMSFs: CoinSpot, Swyftx, Independent Reserve, BTC Markets, Coinstash, CoinJar, Kraken and Cointree, plus OKX since June 2025 and Coinbase since May 2026 ([cryptosmsf.com.au](https://www.cryptosmsf.com.au/guides/best-crypto-exchanges-for-smsf/); [Disruption Banking](https://www.disruptionbanking.com/2025/09/08/coinbase-and-okx-bet-big-on-australias-self-managed-crypto-pensions/)). Only Coinstash advertises same-day SMSF onboarding, and no exchange publishes typical onboarding times. The main comparison guide for these exchanges is published by a seller of SMSF setups that uses affiliate links, and the coin counts and fees on these pages conflict. CoinSpot is listed at 420+, 480+ or 530+ coins, and Kraken's maker fee appears as both 0.25% and 0.00% on one page ([cryptosmsf.com.au](https://www.cryptosmsf.com.au/guides/best-crypto-exchanges-for-smsf/); [coinledger](https://coinledger.io/tools/best-crypto-smsfs), search summary). Koinly says Kraken, Swyftx and Cointree offer business accounts configured for SMSFs rather than dedicated SMSF accounts, which conflicts with Swyftx's own marketing ([Koinly via search snippet](https://koinly.io/blog/crypto-smsf/)).

**The structural point for LibertyStone is that exchanges hold only crypto.** A trustee who holds coins directly runs at least three relationships: the fund bank account, a share broker and one or more exchanges. The alternative is an ASX or Cboe bitcoin ETF bought through the existing broker. VanEck VBTC charges 0.45% a year, DigitalX BTXX 0.49% and Monochrome IBTC 0.25% ([ReviewETF](https://www.reviewetf.com.au/bitcoin-crypto-etfs)). The ETF route needs no wallet or exchange KYC but charges a management fee. Holding directly gives control and round-the-clock trading but leaves custody, records and valuation with the trustee ([ReviewETF blog](https://www.reviewetf.com.au/blog/crypto-etfs-on-the-asx-bitcoin-ethereum-and-everything-in-between)). A platform that holds shares, ETFs and crypto in one place sits between those two routes. Coinbase is pitching to exactly that gap, as discussed below.

## About $5 billion sits in SMSF crypto, but the official figures disagree by quarter

The sector is large and the crypto slice is small. ATO data for the **June 2026 quarter** (published 8 September 2026) counts **680,301 SMSFs, 1,246,552 members and $1.107 trillion in estimated assets**, and 74% of members are aged 50 or over ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/highlights-smsf-quarterly-statistical-report-june-2026)). The crypto figure for the same quarter is **$5,010 million, or 0.45% of total SMSF assets**. That figure was taken from SuperGuide's reproduction of the ATO table and a LinkedIn post citing the same report. The researchers could not open the ATO's xlsx or PDF directly ([SuperGuide, updated 15 Sep 2026](https://www.superguide.com.au/smsfs/smsf-investment-diy-super-asset-types); [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/thomas-prizeman-421245151_for-all-the-noise-about-crypto-in-super-activity-7512987911907135489-UW09)). Earlier reporting gives a much lower series, and the two do not reconcile.

The most likely explanation, which is an inference rather than a confirmed fact, is that the June 2026 report updated its population tables with 2024 to 25 annual return data. SuperGuide also notes that quarterly figures from September 2025 onwards are extrapolated from 2024 to 25 return data rather than drawn from returns lodged for those quarters ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/highlights-smsf-quarterly-statistical-report-june-2026); [SuperGuide](https://www.superguide.com.au/smsfs/smsf-investment-diy-super-asset-types)). Do not mix the roughly $3 billion figures with the roughly $5 billion figures. Quote any number with its quarter, and check it against the ATO spreadsheet before it goes near a script.

The holders are few, but each holds a lot. SuperGuide reports an **average crypto holding of $198,716 and a median of $80,966**. Its figure that "2.4% of all SMSFs had half or more of their assets in crypto" cannot be squared with 2.6% holding any crypto, and probably refers to crypto-holding funds only ([SuperGuide](https://www.superguide.com.au/smsfs/smsf-investment-diy-super-asset-types)). Crypto is concentrated in small funds. It makes up 8.3% of assets in funds of $50k to $100k and 0.3% in funds of $2m to $5m, on 2024 to 25 return data. It is also concentrated in the accumulation phase: 0.8% of accumulation-phase assets against under 0.1% of retirement-phase assets at 30 June 2024 (same source). New members skew younger: 39.1% of new members in the December 2025 quarter were aged 35 to 44 ([autoSMSF](https://www.autosmsf.com.au/quarterly-ato-statistics-update-2026)). Applying 2.2% to 2.6% to 680,301 funds suggests roughly 15,000 to 18,000 crypto-holding SMSFs. That is this report's own arithmetic, mixing an annual share with a quarterly count, and no source gives a fund count. The target market is narrow, which matters under DDO (see the guardrails section). Momentum signals come from vendors. Swyftx says SMSF customers bought 3.36 times as much as they sold in the September 2026 quarter, a record, and that SMSF volume rose 16% quarter on quarter while total exchange volume fell 0.7% ([Cryptonews](https://cryptonews.com.au/news/australian-smsf-investors-push-crypto-buy-sell-ratio-to-record-high-135165/)). These are trading statistics and belong in the media brief, not the ad: on screen they would read as FOMO.

### Coinbase already owns "one place, fewer logins"

Coinbase launched SMSF support on 3 May 2026, backed by its new AFSL ([Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/blog/coinbase-launches-self-managed-super-funds-support-in-australia)). Its Australian managing director framed the product almost exactly as LibertyStone's brief does: "SMSF trustees shouldn't need three logins to manage their retirement" and "they should be able to hold digital assets, equities and other investments in one place" ([SMSF Adviser, 5 May 2026](https://www.smsfadviser.com/coinbase-enters-smsf-sector-with-new-solution/)). Coinbase's self-selected survey found that 80% of respondents were looking to set up new SMSFs and 77% planned to invest up to $100,000, and the launch drew a waitlist of more than 500 ([SMSF Adviser](https://www.smsfadviser.com/coinbase-enters-smsf-sector-with-new-solution/); [CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/coinbase-australia-smsf-crypto-support)). OKX entered in June 2025 and pitched institutional-grade custody ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/okx-arrives-australia-new-crypto-platform)). LibertyStone should therefore avoid "three logins", "one app" and "one place" as the headline joke. Coinbase's audience leans towards people setting up new funds, which is the zone ASIC most distrusts. That leaves LibertyStone room to speak to existing trustees and their accountants about rules, records and the audit, which is also the safer compliance position.

## Auditors, banks and the related-party rule supply the comedy

The audit produces the richest material, because the rules sound absurd when read aloud. In October 2025 the ATO told auditors to confirm that crypto "is stored in a digital wallet in the fund's name". It also told them that "holding statements or investment summaries alone are not sufficient to confirm market value". If existence, ownership or value cannot be verified, both Part A and Part B of the audit report must be qualified where material ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/auditing-smsfs-with-crypto-assets)). One researcher read this page in full. Another received HTTP 403 and saw only snippets, so it is worth confirming once more. In July 2026 the ATO told auditors it would revise its draft crypto guidance to stress control through wallet access and blockchain records. That comes from a search snippet of the [ATO SMSF Auditors stakeholder group key messages, 7 Jul 2026](https://www.ato.gov.au/about-ato/consultation/in-detail/stakeholder-relationship-groups-key-messages/smsf-auditors-professional-association-stakeholder-group/smsf-auditors-professional-association-stakeholder-group-key-messages-7-july-2026), because the page returned 403. Cold wallets are the extreme case. Heffron's Sean Johnston said "I don't think there is a way you can prove existence short of sitting with the auditor". His colleague Leigh Mansell compared them to gold bars at home: "How do you prove to the auditor you physically have those gold bars sitting at home?" ([SMS Magazine, 17 Aug 2023](https://smsmagazine.com.au/?p=32720)). BDO adds that "auditors are increasingly rejecting screenshots and informal records" ([BDO, 27 Nov 2025](https://www.bdo.com.au/en-au/insights/superannuation/a-trustee-s-guide-to-crypto-in-smsfs-be-safe-be-compliant)). One provider puts the valuation rule memorably: "Take the price from a different exchange each year and the auditor will ask why" ([SMSFcentral](https://www.smsfcentral.com.au/smsf-cryptocurrency/)).

**Banks add a second layer of friction.** From June 2022 CBA declined or held some payments to exchanges for 24 hours and capped crypto payments at A$10,000 a calendar month ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/233713/australia-bank-crypto-limits-scams); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/commonwealth-bank-crypto-limits)). In 2023 NAB blocked A$270m in payments to "high-risk" exchanges and told customers that contacting the bank "would not change the outcome" ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/148893/payments-to-high-risk-crypto-exchanges-banned-by-national-australia-bank); [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/national-australia-bank-blocks-a270-million-in-payments-to-high-risk-crypto-exchanges-citing-risk-of-fraud-202307171523)). The per-bank detail for Westpac, ANZ Plus, ING, Macquarie, BOQ and Bankwest, including NAB's "Issuer Declined" message, comes from a single exchange blog whose dates are internally inconsistent. The same blog says reputable Australian exchanges are "generally fine", and its claim that CBA will stop new scheduled payments to exchanges from October 2026 is unconfirmed ([Independent Reserve](https://www.independentreserve.com/blog/knowledge-base/crypto-friendly-banks-australia)). Treat bank friction as real but secondary, and never name a bank in creative.

**Records and accountants complete the picture.** Swyftx, CoinSpot, Kraken and CoinJar offer no adviser or multi-user access, "so the auditor needs exported reports" ([cryptosmsf.com.au](https://www.cryptosmsf.com.au/guides/best-crypto-exchanges-for-smsf/)). A Koinly forum reply says its reports do not apply SMSF tax rates, which leaves that step to the accountant ([Koinly forum](https://discuss.koinly.io/t/smsf-cgt-calculation-in-koinly/24144)). One accountant publishes a "Complex" tier of $1,600 plus GST for bitcoin-holding funds, against $1,300 plus GST for a basic fund ([Resident.com, 31 Aug 2026](https://resident.com/business-and-finance-resources/2026/08/31/5-best-smsf-accountants-for-bitcoin-in-australia-who-publishes-a-crypto-fee)). That is one provider's claim in a commercial ranking, not a market survey. BDO's custody risks give the darker material: lost keys mean "permanent loss of value to the fund", and if the one trustee who knows the keys dies or becomes incapacitated, access may be lost ([BDO](https://www.bdo.com.au/en-au/insights/superannuation/a-trustee-s-guide-to-crypto-in-smsfs-be-safe-be-compliant)). The ATO's 2025 note confirms that trustees have lost crypto through "theft, lost passwords, and impersonation schemes" ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/individuals-and-families/super-for-individuals-and-families/self-managed-super-funds-smsf/smsf-newsroom/navigating-smsf-crypto-assets)).

**Verbatim trustee quotes were not found.** Searches of r/AusFinance, r/fiaustralia, Whirlpool and Facebook groups surfaced no threads. Every first-person quote above comes from a practitioner (Heffron, BDO), not a trustee. Before any trustee wording appears in creative, gather it manually from reddit.com/r/AusFinance and forums.whirlpool.net.au (for example "SMSF crypto", "SMSF auditor crypto", "SMSF Swyftx"), record the URL and date, and get permission or paraphrase. The practitioner quotes can inform the auditor character's voice. Naming Heffron staff, or quoting them on screen, needs their consent and legal review.

## Funny finance ads win when they mock the process, never the asset

The best Australian evidence for funny finance work comes from super funds and banks, not crypto exchanges. **Australian Retirement Trust's "Awaken Your Super" (April 2024)** made super a big blue monster that people must take control of. Trade press reports it delivered 2.9 times the category's average ad attention, and an Effie case study credits a 126% year-on-year ROI increase. Its marketing lead admits the board was not initially on board ([AdNews](https://adnews.com.au/news/how-australian-retirement-trust-s-big-blue-monster-led-to-a-business-boom); [Effies](https://advertisingcouncil.org.au/effies/?p=31041)). **Spirit Super's "Don't Be Super Stupid" (June 2023)** used comedian Alex Ward in 30-second procrastination skits titled "Seance", "Alan" and "Nail" ([Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/?p=239889); [LBB](https://lbbonline.com/profile/Common-Ventures/work)). **ubank's "Cheap Ad, for a Great Rate" (July 2024)** drew its ad in crayon and highlighter, ran sign spinners and human billboards, and kept the rate claim plain. ubank was the most-followed Australian bank on TikTok with 175,400 followers, though no campaign results were published ([The Stable](https://www.thestable.com.au/ubank-cheap-ads-to-woo-australian-savers/)). Rest added a uniformed "Rest Representative" to explain super plainly ([Mumbrella](https://mumbrella.com.au/rest-super-supercharges-brand-platform-with-new-iteration-via-reunion-854386)). Australian crypto exchanges have avoided comedy. Swyftx's 2024 work is aspirational, built around "pursuing simple dreams in big ways" ([AdNews](https://www.adnews.com.au/campaigns/swyftx-unveils-first-campaign-since-brand-refresh-via-landor-and-wavemaker)). That leaves a tonal gap for funny SMSF crypto work, though it also suggests local compliance teams have steered away from humour. No Australian finance ad with verified viral view counts was found.

The international record shows the line clearly. **Coinbase's 2022 Super Bowl QR code** was 60 seconds of a bouncing code with no copy. Coinbase reported more than 20 million site visits in a minute and 445,000 sign-ups, and the app jumped to number two on the US App Store ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/linked/134101); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/02/17/super-bowl-ads-boosted-crypto-app-downloads-by-279-led-by-coinbase/amp)). The play was in the format, and the ad made no claim. The failures share one mechanism: crypto was the hero. Crypto.com's "Fortune Favours the Brave" was ridiculed again after prices fell ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/matt-damon-attacked-after-crypto-bomb/)). FTX's Larry David spot, built around a sceptic who is always wrong, became a liability when FTX collapsed and a class action named David ([AFS Law](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/tom-brady-larry-david-and-others-named-defendants-class-action-suit-filed)). The sharpest warning is Coinbase UK's "Everything Is Fine". The UK Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) upheld 35 complaints in January 2026, finding the cheerful satirical song about cost-of-living misery "trivialised the risks associated with cryptocurrency investment". It banned the ad even though Coinbase argued it was obvious satire ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/356156/uk-regulator-bans-coinbase-ads-over-cost-of-living-messaging); [Trade Informer](https://tradeinformer.com/broker-news/british-watchdog-bans-irresponsible-coinbase-ads-presenting-crypto-as-an-alternative-to-yookay-misery)). Sources date that ad to August 2024 or August 2025. In 2021 the ASA also banned a 20-second Kraken ad that showed its risk warning for one second ([Burges Salmon](https://www.burges-salmon.com/articles/102henu/asa-clamps-down-on-crypto-asset-advertising); [ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/payward-ltd-a21-1121872-payward-ltd.html)). Satire is no defence, and a flashed disclaimer does not count.

Several patterns can be borrowed. Wise's absurdist spots mock outdated money habits, including a banker in the wreckage of an office party paid for by fees ([DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/little-big-engine-wise-be-smart-and-get-wise)). Stake's 1930s-style animation sent up old Wall Street ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/bear-meets-eagle-on-fire-stake-the-takeover-advertising-080222)). Up's gamified TikTok effect showed that "having a sense of humour and being good with money aren't mutually exclusive" ([Mozo](https://www.mozo.com.au/neobanks/articles/up-teams-up-with-tiktok-to-release-australia-s-first-ever-gamified-tiktok-effect)). Read together, the evidence points to one formula. The butt of the joke is admin and process. The humour comes from exaggerating effort. A non-celebrity character carries a series, so no single line can become a meme if prices fall. The last five to seven seconds are given to a straight risk line.

## RG 234, DDO and three ad platforms set the guardrails

**RG 234 contains no section on humour, so comedy is judged on tone, images and overall impression** ([RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)). RG 234.29 requires risk information with "sufficient prominence" against benefits and says "the tone of the advertisement should not undermine the importance of the risks". RG 234.35 and 234.166 to 234.168 say disclaimers must work "on first viewing", including in "Instagram reels or TikTok videos". They also say that viewers "may not pay attention to disclaimers that are barely visible in real time", citing ASIC v Latitude [2024] FCA 1205. RG 234.36 says a link or QR code will not fix a misleading headline. RG 234.145 to 234.148 warn that images of "success, wealth, safety and security" can distract from risk. RG 234.5 extends all of this to AI-generated content, and Hall & Wilcox reads the update as expecting human approval of every AI-made ad ([Hall & Wilcox](https://hallandwilcox.com.au/news/asic-tightens-advertising-guidance-what-financial-and-credit-service-providers-need-to-know/)). That point is directly relevant to an OpenArt-generated spot. ASIC's own examples show which words to avoid. The firm in Example 19 dropped "stress-free" after ASIC objected. Example 17 involved "Options trading is easy" and led to an enforceable undertaking. RG 234.120 to 234.122 caution against "free", "secure" and "guaranteed" ([RG 234 PDF](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)). This creates a creative problem: an ad about how hard SMSF crypto is cannot resolve with "LibertyStone makes it easy". The payoff has to be a concrete, substantiated feature, not ease.

**Advice, disclosure and DDO bite next.** Content that is "reasonably seen as intended to influence" a financial decision is advice ([ASIC INFO 269](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services/giving-financial-product-advice/discussing-financial-products-and-services-online/)). An ad for a product that needs a PDS must say a PDS is available and point to the target market determination (TMD) ([s1018A](https://www5.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca2001172/s1018a.html)). RG 274 says ASIC will check whether campaigns "direct distribution towards the target market". It also says mass-market advertising "generally [is] not appropriate for a product with a narrow target market" ([RG 274](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/etgm1amc/rg274-published-10-september-2024.pdf)). The Kraken operator Bit Trade paid $8m for issuing a crypto margin product with no TMD ([ASIC 24-274MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2024-releases/24-274mr-kraken-crypto-exchange-operator-to-pay-8-million-following-asic-enforcement-action/)). Firstmac paid $8m for cross-marketing outside a TMD, a figure that rests on a search summary ([Broker Daily](https://www.brokerdaily.au/regulation/19883-non-bank-fails-to-meet-ddo-obligations-ordered-to-pay-8m)). The SMSF-specific risk is acute. ASIC warned about marketing that urges Australians to switch "to SMSFs" to buy crypto, and that "SMSF trustees are being targeted to invest in crypto-assets" ([ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/articles/warning-self-managed-super-funds-and-crypto-investments)). Its November 2025 REP 824 found 62 of 100 SMSF establishment advice files failed the best interests duty ([ASIC REP 824](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/reports/rep-824-review-of-smsf-establishment-advice/), search summary). Since February 2026 it has also pursued lead generators in super switching ([ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-02-18/asic-announces-review-into-lead-generators-superannuation/106353740), search summary). On misleading crypto marketing, BPS Financial (Qoin) paid $12m of a $14m penalty for misleading conduct, including implying official approval ([ASIC 26-008MR](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-008mr-bps-financial-to-pay-14-million-in-penalties-over-crypto-qoin-wallet/)). The AANA Code's Practice Note says humour does not excuse negative depiction of a protected attribute such as age. That matters for an audience in which 74% of members are 50 or older ([AANA](https://aana.com.au/self-regulation/code-of-ethics/)).

**The crypto side of a mixed platform has a different legal status.** ASIC's INFO 225 says bitcoin is unlikely to be a financial product, while yield and wrapped-token arrangements can be ([Gilbert + Tobin](https://www.gtlaw.com.au/insights/key-topics/regulation-in-motion/information-sheet-225-info-225)). The Digital Assets Framework Act 2026 received assent on 8 April 2026 and brings platforms into AFS licensing from 9 April 2027 ([Federal Register of Legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2026A00038/asmade/text); [ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/news-items/asics-roadmap-for-digital-assets-law-reform-implementation)). The sources conflict on interim relief. ASIC's roadmap says the INFO 225 no-action position expired in June 2026, while Piper Alderman reports an extension to 30 September 2026 ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/blockchain-bites-high-court-endorses-expansive-financial-product-definitions-in-block-earner-appeal%3B-asic-hits-pause-no-action-relief-for-digital-asset-businesses-extended-to-30-september-2026%3B-ready-or-not-australias-travel-rule-starts-on-1-july/1814008)). Either way, the deadline has passed as of October 2026. The High Court's Block Earner decision of 17 June 2026 held a crypto yield product to be a financial product, so any "earn" or staking feature needs separate review ([MinterEllison](https://www.minterellison.com/articles/high-court-rules-crypto-yield-products-require-an-afsl)).

## Compliance do's and don'ts for a humorous SMSF crypto ad

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Aim the joke at rules, paperwork and process. Personify the rules (the deed, the auditor, the separation rule) | Joke about price, volatility, "to the moon", losses or risk itself. RG 234.29 says tone must not undermine risk ([RG 234](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/cvcjdpy5/rg234-published-09-june-2026.pdf)) |
| Pay off with a concrete, substantiated feature such as "held in your fund's name" or "records your accountant can use" | Say "easy", "simple", "stress-free", "safe", "secure", "guaranteed" or "free" (RG 234 Examples 17 and 19, RG 234.120 to 234.122) |
| Speak to existing SMSF trustees and their accountants | Tell viewers to set up, switch to or "take control" through an SMSF to buy crypto ([ASIC warning](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/articles/warning-self-managed-super-funds-and-crypto-investments); [REP 824](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/reports/rep-824-review-of-smsf-establishment-advice/)) |
| Hold a plain risk line on screen and voice it, in type similar to the main supers, with no gag or animation in the same frame (RG 234.35, 234.37, 234.164, 234.166 to 234.168) | Flash a disclaimer, scroll it fast, or rely on a link or QR code to fix the message (RG 234.36, 234.178; the [ASA Kraken ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/payward-ltd-a21-1121872-payward-ltd.html)) |
| Include a general advice line, the AFSL or authorised representative number, and, if any product needs one, "Consider the PDS and TMD" ([s1018A](https://www5.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca2001172/s1018a.html)) | Show returns, price charts, rising arrows, past performance or Swyftx-style buy/sell momentum statistics (RG 234.81 to 234.88, 234.150) |
| Say plainly that crypto is high risk and may not be a regulated financial product (wording subject to legal review) | Let the regulated shares and ETF side imply licence or safety cover for crypto, or show ATO or ASIC logos (RG 234.128 to 234.132; [Qoin](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-008mr-bps-financial-to-pay-14-million-in-penalties-over-crypto-qoin-wallet/)) |
| Target 18+ and audiences close to trustees and accountants, consistent with the TMD ([RG 274](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/etgm1amc/rg274-published-10-september-2024.pdf)) | Cross-sell "Already holding crypto? Put it in your SMSF", which breaks the related-party rule and follows the Firstmac pattern |
| Cut one crypto version for Meta and TikTok after permission, and one shares and ETF version for YouTube | Run crypto creative on Google or YouTube, or pay TikTok creators to promote the platform ([Google](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/14009787?hl=en); [TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-financial-services)) |
| Cast non-celebrity actors or comedians as characters, and keep trustees roughly 35 to 55 | Mock retirees, "boomers" or confused older people (AANA 2.1, age), swear for laughs (2.5), or use celebrity hype (FTX, Crypto.com) |
| Have a human sign off every AI-generated frame, and keep a record of each ad and its disclaimers (RG 234.5, 234.177) | Generate fake exchange UIs, real bank or competitor logos, lavish lifestyles or fabricated testimonials (RG 234.145 to 234.148, Example 69) |
| Gather trustee wording manually with URLs and permission before quoting it | Quote forum users, Heffron staff or the Coinbase "three logins" line on screen without legal clearance |

## Five comedic concepts that keep the joke on the paperwork

Every concept closes on a shared end card held for at least five seconds: the LibertyStone mark, a substantiated feature line, and a voiced and on-screen risk super. Suggested wording for legal review is: "Crypto is high risk and you could lose all of it. General information only, not advice. Consider your circumstances, your fund's deed and strategy, and the PDS and TMD. LibertyStone [AFSL or AR number]." No gag runs over the end card.

### 1. "The Reading of the Deed" (30 seconds, hero)

**Premise:** A wood-panelled office, mock solemn like a will reading. A lawyer reads the fund's 1998 trust deed aloud to two trustees in their forties. **The joke:** "The trustees may invest in shares... property... and cash." A long pause. A trustee asks, "And the crypto?" The lawyer turns the page, then turns it back, then holds the page up to the light. The super reads: "Trustee Rule No. 1: your deed has to allow it." The scene cuts to the trustees leaving with an updated deed and opening LibertyStone, where shares, ETFs and crypto sit in an account in the fund's name. **Pain point:** number 1, the deed. **Why it stays compliant:** the joke is about a 28-year-old document, not about crypto's prospects. The ad restates an ATO requirement instead of skipping it, it does not tell anyone to start an SMSF, and the payoff is a feature, not ease. Legal should confirm that "your deed has to allow it" reads as factual information rather than advice. The YouTube cut can run with crypto removed by changing the punchline asset to "ETFs", which old deeds may also omit.

### 2. "Not On the List" (15 seconds, social series)

**Premise:** A velvet-rope nightclub labelled "The Smith Family Super Fund". A bouncer in a hi-vis vest carries a clipboard labelled "SIS Act". **The joke:** A coin in sunglasses, labelled "Dad's personal bitcoin", tries to walk in. The bouncer checks the clipboard: "Related party. Not tonight, mate." The coin says, "But I'm family." The bouncer replies, "Exactly." A super reads: "Your fund buys its own, with fund money." **Pain point:** number 2, the related-party ban, with number 3, separation. **Why it stays compliant:** the gag spells out the ATO rule in the punchline, so it teaches rather than misleads. Nothing implies LibertyStone can move personal crypto in. The ad shows no price or returns, and the 15 seconds include the held risk line. If that line cannot be read in time, RG 234.175 says to lengthen the cut or change the medium.

### 3. "Show and Tell" (30 seconds, hero or two 15-second cutdowns)

**Premise:** A primary-school show-and-tell staged in an accountant's office. The auditor sits cross-legged, and adult trustees take turns presenting. **The joke:** The first trustee holds up a printed screenshot. The auditor gently stamps it "NOT SUFFICIENT". The second opens a shoebox holding a hardware wallet wrapped in a sock: "It's definitely the fund's. It's got a sticker." The auditor asks, "Can I see inside?" The trustee hugs the box. The third trustee hands over a fund-name statement and transaction export from LibertyStone, and the auditor nods and reaches for a calculator, signalling there is still work to do. **Pain point:** numbers 4 and 5, existence and ownership evidence and rejected screenshots. **Why it stays compliant:** the ATO says holding statements alone are not sufficient, so the ending deliberately stops short of "audit done" or "audit-ready". It shows records that help, not a guaranteed sign-off. The auditor is competent, not mocked, and no real person's quote is used. Every on-screen document is a designed mock-up checked for legibility, not generated text.

### 4. "The Thirtieth of June" (20 to 30 seconds, seasonal, May to July flight)

**Premise:** It is 11:58 pm on 30 June, shot like a heist thriller with ticking strings. A trustee has three monitors open for the bank, the broker and an exchange, plus a sticky note reading "same exchange as last year??". **The joke:** The phone rings and the accountant whispers, "I need the closing price. From the same place as last year." The trustee scrolls frantically through three tabs. The clock strikes midnight and a CSV prints out like a receipt that runs off the desk and out the door. The scene cuts to the following year: the same trustee asleep at 11:58 pm, with the LibertyStone record already in the accountant's inbox. **Pain point:** numbers 7 and 8, the 30 June valuation and the accountant's exports. **Why it stays compliant:** the scene dramatises record keeping and avoids the "fewer logins" headline Coinbase owns. It makes no claim about the price itself. The year-two payoff must match what LibertyStone actually provides, such as a consistent 30 June AUD valuation and an export the accountant can access. If the platform's valuation source is not independent, the copy must not imply it replaces the auditor's own price evidence.

### 5. "Only Gary Knows" (15 seconds, social series)

**Premise:** A two-trustee fund meeting held as formal minutes, with a gavel, at a backyard barbecue table. **The joke:** "Item four: where is the seed phrase?" Both trustees slowly turn to look at Gary, a co-trustee on a fishing trip, seen as a distant figure in a tinnie with no reception, waving. **Pain point:** number 9, key-person risk. **Why it stays compliant:** the humour sits in the meeting format and Gary's absence, not in losing money, and no loss is shown. The payoff line ("Held in the fund's name, with access for every trustee") is used only if the platform supports multi-trustee access. The ad must not imply custody risk disappears, because the BDO and ATO material on theft and lost passwords is the reason a risk line is needed. Cast Gary as middle-aged, so the gag is about distance, not age.

The concepts work as a series. The rules become recurring characters (the Deed, the Bouncer, the Auditor, the Clock), and "Trustee Rule No. X" is a running device. That gives LibertyStone a comic universe Coinbase's "one place" message does not occupy, and it suits the one-pain-point-per-cut structure that worked for Spirit Super's 30-second skits ([Campaign Brief](https://campaignbrief.com/?p=239889)).

## Evidence gaps and items that need legal review

Several facts rest on search summaries or snippets rather than opened primary pages. They are: the "shares, property and cash" deed wording (Kalkine, Grow SMSF); the Koinly business-account claim; Swyftx's SMSF page details; the ATO auditors' stakeholder key messages of 7 July 2026; REP 824 figures; ASIC's lead-generator review; the Firstmac and Amex penalties; Google's Australian verification page; and the AUSTRAC transitional dates. The ATO's core SMSF crypto guidance was read from an API copy dated 2022 because the HTML page returned 404. The bank caps rely on one exchange blog with inconsistent dates. The June 2026 crypto figure comes from secondary reproductions of the ATO table. The table above records the statistical conflicts by quarter.

Legal review is needed on these points: whether LibertyStone's platform requires a PDS or TMD, which determines whether s1018A and DDO targeting apply; whether s912F requires the licence number in ads (it could not be opened); the regulatory status of the crypto side, and whether ASIC's no-action relief applies after 30 September 2026; whether misleading crypto claims fall under the ASIC Act or the Australian Consumer Law on a mixed platform; whether lines such as "your deed has to allow it" amount to advice; the exact risk-warning wording; Meta and TikTok crypto permissions; and any use of named practitioners' quotes. No Ad Standards case on a humorous crypto or investment ad was found, and that database should be searched directly.

## Conclusion

For SMSF crypto, compliance and comedy point the same way. The rules that make SMSF crypto hard are the funniest material available, and an ad that dramatises them restates the ATO's requirements. That keeps it on the right side of RG 234 and the "don't push people into SMSFs" warning, provided the payoff is a substantiated feature and never "now it's easy". The banned Coinbase UK ad showed that satire does not protect a joke that makes crypto the escape. The ART monster and Spirit Super skits showed that a character-led series about admin can be measured and approved. LibertyStone therefore competes on tone and audience rather than on the "one place" proposition Coinbase already uses: existing trustees, their accountants and the annual audit, with the risk line delivered straight.

Two constraints will shape the media plan more than the creative. Google's ban on crypto exchange ads means the crypto theme cannot run on YouTube. DDO and a target market of perhaps 15,000 to 18,000 crypto-holding funds argue against broad viral reach and for tightly targeted Meta and TikTok placements, with accountants as a second audience. Before scripting begins, confirm the June 2026 ATO figure, collect real trustee wording, and get LibertyStone's licensing and feature claims in writing.
